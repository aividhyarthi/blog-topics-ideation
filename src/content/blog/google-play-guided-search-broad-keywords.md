**Key points:**
- Google Play's [Guided Search](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-play-store-new-spaces-guided-search-announced-3600047/) intercepts broad queries and shows AI-organized subcategories before the plain ranked results list.
- These AI results carry an "Organized with AI" label; regular, non-AI results still appear below under "All results."
- It rolled out from September 23, 2025, confirmed independently by both [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-play-store-new-spaces-guided-search-announced-3600047/) and [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/09/23/google-play-you-gaming-profile/).
- Ranking #1 for a broad term still counts. It just no longer guarantees the first thing a searcher sees.

Search "fitness apps" on Google Play today. You may not land on a plain ranked list right away. Google can show you a set of AI-picked subcategories first. Things like "yoga apps for beginners" or "running trackers with heart rate." Each comes with a small preview of top results. The old, familiar ranked list is still there. It's just not always first in line anymore.

## What Guided Search actually does

Guided Search catches broad, generic queries and reorganizes them. Instead of one long list ranked top to bottom, it groups results into narrower subcategories. Each group gets a short preview. Google marks this whole section "Organized with AI." Scroll past it, and the regular ranked list is still there. It's just labeled "All results" now.

This isn't a ranking algorithm change in the usual sense. It's a new layer sitting on top of search. That layer decides which subcategory a broad query gets routed into. Your rank only gets seen after that.

| Query type | What a searcher sees first |
|---|---|
| Broad ("fitness apps") | AI-organized subcategories, each a mini preview |
| Specific ("yoga apps for beginners") | The regular ranked list |

## Why this matters for your keyword list

Say your ASO strategy leans on ranking #1 for a handful of big, broad keywords. That rank now competes with an AI shortcut most searchers see first. A fitness app at #1 for "fitness apps" still shows up in the "All results" list eventually. But a searcher steered into "yoga apps for beginners" by Guided Search may never scroll that far down.

The shift is simple. Broad-keyword rank still counts. It just isn't the whole game anymore. Covering the narrower, specific phrases real users actually type now matters more than it used to.

> **Real-world scenario:** A budgeting app ranked #2 for "budget apps," a genuinely broad, high-volume term the team had spent months chasing. After Guided Search rolled out, most traffic on that broad query got routed into subcategories like "budget apps for couples" and "budget apps with bill reminders" before reaching the plain list. The app wasn't ranked at all for either specific phrase. Fixing that took adding those exact phrases to the keyword list and coverage tracking, not chasing the broad term harder.

## What to actually check

1. **Don't drop your broad keywords.** They still matter for the "All results" list underneath Guided Search's picks.
2. **Add narrower, intent-specific phrases to your tracked list.** These are the sub-queries Guided Search is likely to generate from your broad terms, and the ones a searcher may see before yours.
3. **Track more than your top 5-10 keywords.** A keyword-universe view that covers dozens of narrower variants gives you a shot at showing up in whichever subcategory Guided Search routes a searcher into.
