**Key points:**
- Unpublishing hides your app from new users. It doesn't erase the app's identity or its past.
- Your existing ratings, reviews, and install history all stay tied to the same app package.
- Republish the same app, and that same history comes right back. There's no fresh start.
- Treating unpublish and republish as a reset is a common guess that just doesn't match how app identity works.

Taking a struggling app down and bringing it back up sounds like a reset button. It isn't one.

## What actually happens

Unpublishing removes your app from new searches and new installs. What it doesn't do is erase your app's identity. The same package, tied to the same developer account, keeps its full history attached the whole time it's hidden. Republish that same app, and you're not making a new listing from scratch. You're just turning visibility back on, with the same history riding along underneath it the entire time.

| What people assume | What actually happens |
|---|---|
| Unpublishing wipes the slate clean | The app's identity and history stay right where they were |
| Republishing starts fresh | The same ratings, reviews, and history come right back |
| It's a reset tool | It's a visibility switch, nothing more |

## Why teams reach for it anyway

A run of bad reviews or a bad bug can make a fresh start feel tempting. Pull the app, fix things quietly, then bring it back with a clean record. That sounds like a fair way to leave a rough patch behind. The problem is the slate was never actually clean. The same package carries its own past forward no matter how long it sat hidden. A team hoping for a true reset ends up right back where it started, just after losing visibility for a while in between.

> **Real-world scenario:** A team facing a wave of bad reviews after a rough release pulled their app for two weeks, planning to fix the real issues and come back with a cleaner record. When they republished, their old rating average and review history came right back, exactly as it had been. Nothing had reset. They'd lost two weeks of new installs during the gap, and the thing they'd hoped to leave behind, the bad reviews already on file, was still sitting there waiting. Fixing the app itself afterward helped going forward. Unpublishing never touched the past they wanted gone.

## When unpublishing is actually the right move

There's a real, fair use for this tool. A serious bug that's actively hurting users is a good reason to pull an app for a short stretch while a fix ships, so fewer people hit the same problem. A real business reason to pause for a while is fair too. What it isn't is a way to shed a bad rating, erase old reviews, or fake a fresh start. Those things stay attached to the app, whether it's visible or not.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Don't unpublish hoping for a reset.** Your rating, reviews, and history travel with the same package the whole time, visible or not.
2. **Use it to actually contain a problem, not to hide a track record.** Pausing while you fix a real, active bug is a fair use. Hoping for a clean slate isn't.
3. **Fix the real issue instead of hiding from it.** The history bothering you stays exactly where it was once you come back.
4. **Weigh the real cost of the gap.** Unpublishing has a genuine downside, fewer new installs while hidden, in exchange for a reset that never actually happens.
