**Key points:**
- A verified purchase tag confirms a real transaction happened. It says nothing about the review's actual content or quality.
- It generally doesn't change how a review counts toward your overall rating average. A rating is a rating either way.
- What it does change is reader trust, since a verified tag tells someone the opinion came from a real user.
- The tag's real effect lands on human judgment, not on the math behind your score.

A small badge next to a review feels like it should mean that review counts for more. What it actually changes is smaller than that.

## What the tag actually confirms

A verified purchase tag confirms one fact. This reviewer genuinely downloaded or bought the app through the store, instead of writing about it from the outside. It says nothing about whether the review is well written, fair, detailed, or useful. It's a transaction check, not a quality check. A glowing, thoughtful review and a one-line complaint can both carry the same verified tag, since the tag only confirms the purchase happened.

| | What the tag confirms | What it doesn't confirm |
|---|---|---|
| Verified purchase | A real download or purchase happened | Review quality, fairness, or usefulness |

## Why it's easy to assume it changes the math

A tag that signals extra trust feels like it should come with extra weight baked into your rating average. Store ratings usually don't work that way. A rating is a rating, counted the same whether it carries a verified tag or not. The tag sits next to the review as a trust signal for human readers. It isn't an input that changes how your score is calculated.

> **Real-world scenario:** A team noticed a cluster of strong, detailed reviews that happened to carry the verified tag and wondered if that cluster was pulling extra weight in their overall rating, given how often the tag showed up. Checking the actual math behind individual reviews, verified and unverified ratings were counting the same toward the average. What the tag was actually doing was making those specific reviews more convincing to readers browsing the listing. A real effect, just not the one the team had first assumed.

## Where the real effect actually lands

The real impact of a verified tag sits in human judgment, not in your rating math. A reader deciding whether to trust one review is more likely to lean on a verified one, since it removes a real doubt. Was this person actually a user. That's a real, meaningful effect. It just works through persuasion and trust, not through the math your rating is built from.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Don't assume verified reviews carry extra weight in your average.** The tag confirms a purchase, not a different calculation.
2. **See the tag's real value as reader trust, not rating math.** A verified review is more convincing to a human reader, and that matters on its own.
3. **Pay attention to what verified reviews actually say.** A strong, detailed verified review does more real persuasive work than the tag alone gives it credit for.
4. **Don't write off unverified feedback as worthless.** It counts just the same in your real rating, even if it reads as less convincing to a browsing user.
