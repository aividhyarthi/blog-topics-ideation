**Key points:**
- Google's own October 2026 system release notes, tied to Google Play Store version 53.8 (dated October 26, 2026), confirm Ask Play responses can now include relevant videos, shown with citations and timestamps.
- Multiple outlets reporting on the same release notes agree on this detail, including Android Authority, which says users will "soon" start seeing it.
- Google's own notes don't explain how videos get selected or verified before being cited in a response.
- No universal rollout schedule has been published, so this reads as rolling out, not uniformly live.

A chat answer in the Play Store just got a new kind of receipt. A video. Timestamped. Cited right in the response.

## What Google's own release notes actually confirm

Per Google's own October 2026 release notes, tied to Play Store version 53.8, Ask Play can now cite real videos. With timestamps. That's a real, dated, official confirmation. Not just a feature spotted in a teardown.

| Detail | What's confirmed |
|---|---|
| Change | Ask Play responses can cite videos, with timestamps |
| Tied to | Google Play Store v53.8, dated October 26, 2026 |
| Source | Google's own system release notes |
| Selection process | Not explained in Google's own notes |
| Rollout schedule | Not published |

## Why the timestamp detail actually matters

A citation alone tells a user a claim came from somewhere. A timestamp tells them exactly where to look. No scrubbing through a whole video to check if the summary was fair. That's a real jump from a plain text citation. It turns a video into something a user can check in seconds. The same way a page number beats just a book title.

> **Real-world scenario:** A fitness app had a popular YouTube walkthrough of its workout builder. It sat buried seven minutes into a longer video most people would never sit through. After this update, a user asking Ask Play about workout customization got a direct link to that same video. Timestamped right to the moment the builder appeared on screen. The team hadn't changed a thing. Their old video simply became useful to a chat answer it was never built to feed.

## What Google's own notes leave out

No explanation of how a video gets picked or checked before Ask Play cites it. That's a real gap if you want your own videos to show up this way. There's no submission process yet. No optimization checklist either. Google hasn't described one. Treat any advice on "optimizing for Ask Play video citations" as a guess, until Google actually documents how this works.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Check if your app already has video that could plausibly get cited.** A YouTube walkthrough. A demo reel. Anything showing real app behavior.
2. **Don't chase a formal process that doesn't exist yet.** Google's own notes describe the feature. Not a way to request inclusion.
3. **Check your video's clarity at specific moments, not just overall quality.** A good citation likely needs a feature clearly visible at one exact point. Not just mentioned in passing.
4. **Watch for Google's own follow-up notes.** The selection method is the real open question. Google is more likely to explain it over time than leave it undocumented forever.
