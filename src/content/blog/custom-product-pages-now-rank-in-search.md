**Key points:**
- Apple's own [custom product pages page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/custom-product-pages/) states plainly that custom pages can appear in search results.
- The same page confirms you can assign keywords to a page. That page then shows up for those keywords, in place of your default page.
- Apple's own docs confirm you can publish up to 70 extra custom product pages.
- Each keyword can only go to one page at a time, per Apple's own rule. So pages can't compete against each other for the same search.

Custom product pages used to be an ad tool first. Organic search was an afterthought at best. Apple's own docs now say something new. A custom page can show up in real search results. Assign it keywords, and that exact page can win that exact search. That's a real shift in what the tool is for.

## What Apple's own docs actually say

The line is direct, not implied. Apple's [custom product pages page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/custom-product-pages/) states: "Custom product pages can also appear in relevant search results." It goes further. It says you can assign keywords to a custom page. This lets that page show up for those keywords, instead of your default page.

That second part is the real change. A search for one keyword can now land on a page built just for that intent. Its own screenshots. Its own promo text. Its own preview video. Not the one general page every other search also lands on.

## The page limit just doubled too

Apple's own docs confirm the new cap. You can publish up to 70 extra custom pages. That's real room to build pages for a wide spread of search terms. Not just two or three pages, hoping they cover enough ground.

One real rule to know first. Apple's guidance says each keyword can only point to one page. You can't hand the same keyword to two pages and let them fight it out in search.

| Before this change | After this change |
|---|---|
| Custom pages mainly served ad campaigns | Custom pages can appear in plain organic search |
| One default page covered every search | Specific keywords can route to a specific page |
| Up to 35 custom pages | Up to 70 custom pages |

## Why this matters even without ads

It's easy to file custom pages under "ad tool" and move on. That filing is now out of date. This keyword-to-page link is an organic search feature. Apple's own page confirms it. It works with zero ad spend behind it.

> **Real-world scenario:** A meditation app built its one default page around its top feature: guided sleep sessions. But a good chunk of real search traffic wanted something else. Quick stress relief during the workday. The team built a second, custom page around that exact use case. New screenshots. New promo text. Then they gave that page the keywords people actually typed for that need. Those searchers landed on a page built for what they wanted, not a page built around a different feature entirely.

## What to actually do with this

1. **Look for distinct search intents, not just distinct keywords.** A custom page earns its place when your default page doesn't speak to that intent at all.
2. **Read Apple's own rule before you assign keywords.** Each one can only point to a single page under current rules.
3. **Stop treating this as an ads-only tool.** Build for real search intent even with zero paid campaigns running.
4. **Use the room you now have.** Up to 70 pages is real space for meaningfully different searches, not just a couple of seasonal copies.
