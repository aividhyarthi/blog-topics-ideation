**Key points:**
- Featuring is an editorial pick made by store staff. You can't buy it or ask for it directly.
- Sponsored placement is paid. You control it with a budget and a bid, not editorial approval.
- The two run on separate tracks. Paying for one doesn't touch the other at all.
- Both can genuinely grow your reach, but they call for different plans, since only one is in your direct control.

Getting seen more can happen two different ways, and only one of them is something you can just switch on.

## What splits the two apart

Featuring is a human, editorial call. Someone on the store's team picks your app for a curated spot, usually tied to quality, design, or a theme the store is pushing that week. You can't buy your way in, and you usually can't ask for it directly either. Sponsored placement runs on the flip side. You set a budget and a bid, and the spot is yours for as long as you keep paying for it. No editorial judgment on your end at all.

| | Featured | Sponsored |
|---|---|---|
| How you get it | An editorial pick by store staff | A paid budget and bid |
| Can you buy it directly? | No | Yes |
| Can you request it? | Not directly | Yes, by setting up a campaign |

## Why mixing them up leads to the wrong plan

Treating featuring like something you can buy or request wastes effort chasing a lever that isn't actually yours to pull. Treating sponsored placement like it needs the same polish and pitch as a featured ask can mean overthinking a system that mostly just needs a working budget and a fair bid. The two call for real different plans. Only one responds directly to spend. The other responds to something closer to overall quality and timing, which you can't fully control or predict.

> **Real-world scenario:** A team spent weeks polishing their app's visuals and writing a pitch, hoping to land a featured spot, while treating a paid sponsored campaign as a lower priority for later. The featured spot never came, since editorial picks aren't something a pitch can reliably force. Meanwhile, the sponsored campaign they'd put off would have started driving real, steady traffic the moment they turned it on. They'd put urgent effort into the system they couldn't control, and left the one they could control sitting idle.

## What actually works for each

Keep building a genuinely strong, well-made app. That's the real path toward ever getting featured, even with no direct way to ask. For sponsored placement, treat it as a lever you can pull whenever your budget and goals call for it, since it answers to spend and setup, not editorial favor. Don't let hope for one distract you from action on the other.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Stop trying to buy or directly request a featured spot.** It's an editorial call, and your time is better spent on real app quality than on a pitch.
2. **Use sponsored placement as a direct, controllable lever.** It answers to budget and setup, which makes it the one you can actually act on right now.
3. **Don't assume paying for one buys the other.** Sponsored spend doesn't move editorial featuring, and strong organic rank doesn't hand you free sponsored placement either.
4. **Keep building real quality as your long path toward featuring.** It's the only lever that genuinely moves that needle, even with no direct ask.
