**Key points:**
- Not seeing your own ad when you search Play Store yourself usually isn't a bug. App campaigns don't guarantee every search shows it.
- A newly published or recently un-suspended app can take 24 to 48 hours before ads are eligible to run at all.
- New or edited ad assets go through a review, typically finishing within a day or two. Editing mid-review restarts that clock.
- A limited budget or a thin set of ad assets means fewer auctions your ad can even enter, Play Store search included.

You built a Google Ads app campaign. Your app is live and active. You search for it in the Play Store yourself, and no ad shows up. Here's what to actually check, in the order it matters.

## Check these first, in order

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| App status | Live, not suspended, no policy flags |
| Campaign age | Under 24 to 48 hours old often means not eligible yet |
| Ad review status | Under review, disapproved, or approved |
| Budget vs. bid | Budget should be well above your target cost-per-install |
| Asset count | Few creative assets limits how many auctions you enter |

## Why your own search isn't a reliable test

App campaigns run across several Google surfaces at once: Play Store search, Google Search, YouTube, Discover, and Display. Your ad competing in an auction for one specific search, at one specific moment, from your own device, isn't the same as your ad simply not running. Placement in any single auction depends on your bid, your ad's quality signals, and who else is bidding on that exact query right then.

A newly published app, or one that was just un-suspended, often isn't available in Google Ads right away. Give it 24 to 48 hours before assuming something's broken. The same window applies to a freshly created campaign or one with recently edited creative assets. Editing an ad or asset while it's under review restarts that review clock, so repeated small edits can keep a campaign stuck in limbo longer than it needs to be.

Budget matters more than most people expect here too. App install campaigns using target cost-per-install bidding need a daily budget well above the bid itself, since the system needs enough auction attempts to actually learn what's working. A budget too close to your bid limits how many auctions your ad is even eligible to enter.

> **Real-world scenario:** A team launched a new app install campaign and searched their own app name in Play Store the next morning, expecting to see their ad. Nothing showed. Rather than assume the campaign was broken, they checked the campaign's age (under 24 hours), its ad review status (still pending), and its daily budget (set close to the target cost-per-install). They waited a full 48 hours without making further edits, and their budget cleared the recommended threshold. The ad began appearing in search results within the next day, without them changing anything else.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Wait at least 24 to 48 hours on a new or recently changed campaign.** Most "it's not showing" reports resolve on their own in that window.
2. **Check ad review status before assuming the campaign is broken.** A pending review is not a failure. It's just not done yet.
3. **Set your budget well above your target cost-per-install.** A budget too close to the bid limits how many auctions your ad can enter.
4. **Add more creative assets if you're only running one or two.** More assets mean more auction eligibility across every surface, Play Store search included.
