**Key points:**
- A real thread on [Apple's own developer forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/65721) states plainly that changing category unlists you from the old one, then starts you fresh in the new one.
- Apple's [App Store Connect reference](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/) explains how to set your category. It says nothing about what a later switch does to your rank.
- A second [real forum thread](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/94134) shows this same question coming up again. Not a one-off developer complaint.
- This is a real gap between what Apple documents and what developers actually report living through.

Category rank feels like a small setting buried in App Store Connect. Change it, and developer forums say something bigger happens than a label swap. Worth knowing exactly what's confirmed here. And what's just widely reported experience.

## What developers have actually reported

A [real thread on Apple's own developer forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/65721) says this plainly. Change your category, and you get fully unlisted from the old one. Your rank there is gone. You start over in the new category from zero. A [second real thread](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/94134) shows another developer asking the same question later. Not a one-off. A recurring, real worry among people who've made the switch, or thought about it.

## What Apple's own documentation actually covers

Apple's [App Store Connect reference](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/) explains how Primary and Secondary Category work as a setting. Clear, confirmed, official. What it skips entirely is the rank cost of switching one later. No stated reset rule. No stated safe path either. That's a real, honest gap in Apple's own guidance. Not something made up for this post.

## Why this matters before you touch the setting

A category switch usually comes from a real business reason. Your app outgrew its old category. A competitor's category pulls more search traffic. Whatever the reason, treat this as a real, weighty decision. The upside is a fresh, maybe bigger audience. The reported downside is losing rank built over months or years. A real trade worth thinking through first. Not a setting to flip on a whim.

> **Real-world scenario:** A meditation app had ranked steadily in Health & Fitness for over a year. The team weighed a switch to Lifestyle, guessing it was broader and less crowded. Before deciding, they read Apple's own developer forums. They found the same reset pattern reported by several other developers. So they stayed in Health & Fitness. Instead, they put that effort into a secondary category tag. Their rank stayed fully intact. A smaller move, with none of the reported reset risk.

## What to actually do before switching category

1. **Read Apple's own developer forum threads first.** The pattern is reported again and again, even with no official confirmation.
2. **Try a secondary category change before touching your primary one.** A smaller, lower-risk way to reach a new audience.
3. **Don't expect an official rollback path if the reset happens.** Nothing documented promises one.
4. **Track your rank closely for weeks after any change.** Real data on your own app beats a forum post about someone else's.
