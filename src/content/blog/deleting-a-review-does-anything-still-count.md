**Key points:**
- A review that's really deleted comes out of your rating average, the same as if it never existed.
- Deleting usually doesn't use up a one-time chance. A user can typically leave a fresh review later if they want.
- Deleting differs from changing a rating to something neutral. One pulls the review out of your average entirely. The other keeps it counted at a new value.
- The clean removal that deletion implies is mostly true, which makes it a genuinely different act from editing or lowering a rating.

A deleted review feels like it should vanish without a trace. For once, that's close to how it actually works.

## What deletion actually removes

Once a review is deleted, it drops out of the pool your rating average is built from. Your average recalculates as if that review had never been sent in at all. This is one of the more simple parts of how reviews work. Deletion really does act like the clean removal it looks like. Some other review actions look final but aren't.

| | What happens to your average |
|---|---|
| Review deleted | Comes out of the pool entirely, average recalculates without it |
| Review changed to a lower rating | Still counted, just at a new value |

## Why this is worth telling apart from a similar-looking action

A user changing their rating to something low or neutral can look, at a glance, like they've walked back their review. That's a different act from deletion. A changed rating is still sitting in your average, just with a different number than before. Only a real deletion pulls the review out of that math entirely. Mixing the two up can lead to misreading what's actually happening to your numbers.

> **Real-world scenario:** A team noticed their rating average shift and first assumed a batch of unhappy users had simply lowered their ratings, expecting those reviews to still be sitting there, visible, with a worse number attached. Checking closer, several of those reviews had actually been deleted outright, not just edited down. The effect on the average turned out similar either way that time, but the team had misread which act had actually happened. That mix-up would have mattered more if they'd also tried to read or reply to the review text, text that was simply gone once deleted.

## Why deletion doesn't close the door for good

A user who deletes their review usually isn't using up a one-time shot. They can usually come back and leave a fresh review later if their mind changes, or they just feel like it. Deletion removes the specific review that existed, not any future chance to write a new one. Worth knowing if you're ever tempted to treat a deleted review as a closed, final chapter with that specific user.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Treat a deleted review as genuinely gone from your average.** This is one of the more literal, true parts of how reviews actually work.
2. **Don't mix up deletion with a rating simply lowered.** One pulls the review out of your average entirely. The other keeps it counted at a new value.
3. **Remember a user can usually leave a new review after deleting an old one.** Deletion isn't a permanently shut door on that user.
4. **Check which act actually happened before drawing conclusions from a rating shift.** The difference matters more than it looks at first glance.
