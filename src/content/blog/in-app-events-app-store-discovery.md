In-App Events are one of the App Store's more concrete discovery tools, and also one of the most under-used, mostly because it's not obvious what they actually do beyond "a nice card on your listing." Here's the honest scope: what they are, where they can show up, and what to actually expect from using them.

## What an In-App Event actually is

A structured, time-bound promotion for something happening inside your app, submitted through App Store Connect: a title, image, event dates, a badge (Premiere, Live Now, Coming Soon, or Cash Prize/Challenge for games), and a deep link straight into that experience. It's not a generic marketing banner, Apple's guidelines require the event to be a real, scheduled thing happening in the app during the stated window, not an evergreen feature dressed up as an event.

## Where events can actually surface

| Surface | What it looks like | How you get there |
|---|---|---|
| Your product page | A dedicated event card at the top | Automatic once approved |
| Search results | An event card can appear alongside your app in relevant search | Automatic, if Apple's system judges it relevant |
| Today tab | A full editorial feature | Editorially selected, not guaranteed |
| Games/Apps tab, "Events" browse | A featured slot | Editorially selected |

The product-page card is close to guaranteed once an event is approved. Everything past that, search surfacing and any editorial placement, is Apple's system and editorial team deciding relevance and quality, not something submission alone controls.

## What events realistically do for you

Nothing here is a documented direct ranking lever, the way Android vitals thresholds are on Google Play. What events add is genuine extra visibility surface: a reason to appear in search results beyond your evergreen listing, a shot at Today tab placement that reaches users who weren't searching for your category at all, and a fresh reason for lapsed users to reopen the app. That traffic and engagement can feed the factors that are more directly tied to ranking (installs, retention, engagement signal) without the event itself being a named ranking input.

> **Real-world scenario:** A fitness app ran its first In-App Event for a two-week step-count challenge, submitted with a clear premise and clean creative. It didn't get Today tab placement, but it did surface on search for several fitness-adjacent terms it hadn't shown up for before, and re-engaged roughly 8% of lapsed users who opened the app specifically for the event. Keyword rank for its core terms didn't move measurably during the window, but the re-engagement lift was real and measurable on its own, which is closer to what events actually deliver than a rank bump.

## Making an event worth submitting

1. **Make it a genuine event, not a rebadged feature.** Apple's review process checks for a real scheduled activity, and a generic "check out our app" card reads poorly to users even if it clears review.
2. **Time it to something real**, a seasonal moment, a content drop, a live competition, rather than an arbitrary two-week window with nothing actually changing in the app.
3. **Treat the creative like a store listing asset**, not an afterthought. The image and title are what earns the tap in search and browse, the same principles that apply to [screenshot and icon testing](/blog/app-icon-ab-testing) apply here.
4. **Track engagement and re-open rate during the window**, not keyword rank alone. If the goal is re-engagement and extra visibility surface rather than a direct rank lever, measure it against that goal.

In-App Events aren't a ranking hack, they're a real, if editorially gated, extra visibility channel. Worth using when you have a genuine event to promote, not worth forcing for the sake of having one on your listing.
