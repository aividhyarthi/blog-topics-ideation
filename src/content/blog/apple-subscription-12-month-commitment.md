**Key points:**
- Apple's own [developer news post](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=agq42lxe) confirms monthly subscriptions with a 12-month commitment. Now configurable in App Store Connect.
- Subscribers get a lower monthly rate for a 12-month term. Not a single upfront annual charge.
- Canceling stops future renewal once the 12 payments are done. It does not cancel payments already owed.
- Available worldwide except the United States and Singapore, per Apple's own page. Live from iOS 26.4. Required from iOS 26.5 in May.

A subscriber who wants annual pricing, without an annual invoice, now has a real option. Apple's own page confirms it. The mechanics matter more than the headline.

## What Apple's own page confirms

[Apple's own developer news](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=agq42lxe) describes it in plain terms. A monthly subscription with a 12-month commitment gives a lower monthly rate. In exchange, the subscriber agrees to a full 12-month term. Apple also builds in visible tracking. A subscriber sees completed and remaining payments right in their Apple Account. Apple sends an email, plus a push notification if opted in, ahead of each renewal.

| Detail | What Apple's own page confirms |
|---|---|
| Billing style | Monthly charges, tied to a 12-month term |
| Early cancellation | Stops future renewal, doesn't erase remaining committed payments |
| Payment visibility | Shown in the subscriber's Apple Account |
| Reminder system | Email, plus push if opted in |
| Availability | Worldwide except US and Singapore |
| Minimum OS | iOS 26.4 and equivalents, required by iOS 26.5 |

## Why the cancellation mechanics are the real story

The headline reads like a discount. The mechanics work more like a phone carrier contract. A subscriber who cancels in month four doesn't walk away free. Future renewal stops only once they've finished the payments they already agreed to. That's a real, different shape from a normal auto-renewing plan. There, canceling this month means no charge next month.

For a developer, this changes what "churn" even measures for this one plan. A canceled 12-month subscriber may still be a paying, billed subscriber for months after they hit cancel. Track that cohort like a standard monthly plan, and your real revenue and retention numbers will read wrong.

> **Real-world scenario:** A fitness app added the 12-month commitment option right after Apple's own page confirmed it. Their team tracked it in the same churn dashboard as their standard monthly plan, at first. A batch of commitment subscribers canceled early. The dashboard flagged a churn spike. Checking Apple's own mechanics showed the real picture. Those subscribers still owed their remaining payments. They hadn't actually stopped paying yet. The team built a separate view for commitment subscribers instead. It tracked completed versus remaining payments. Not a cancel tap as an instant loss.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Read Apple's own mechanics before you add this plan.** Cancellation here doesn't work like a normal auto-renewing subscription. Know the gap before you price around it.
2. **Track commitment subscribers on their own.** A canceled commitment subscriber can still be actively paying. Mix that into one churn number, and it hides what's really going on.
3. **Check your own market against the availability list.** Not available in the US or Singapore, per Apple's own page. Confirm your target markets before you build marketing around it.
4. **Confirm your minimum OS target.** Setup needs iOS 26.4 or later. Full rollout needs iOS 26.5 in May. A large pre-26.4 user base won't see this option yet.
