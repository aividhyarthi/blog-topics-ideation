**Key points:**
- Apple's own [DMA compliance page](https://developer.apple.com/support/dma-and-apps-in-the-eu/) confirms alternative marketplaces, direct web distribution, and app notarization as real, current options in the EU.
- New Apple business terms for EU developers take effect October 1, 2026. A real, confirmed date, not a rumor.
- Apple documents alternative distribution and App Store search as separate systems. Neither Apple page found states one blocks the other outright.
- This is genuinely new and fast-moving. Confirm your own specific setup with Apple directly rather than relying on general blog advice, this one included.

The EU's Digital Markets Act changed what's possible for app distribution there. Alternative marketplaces. Direct web downloads. A real menu of options that didn't exist a few years back. What's less clear to a lot of developers is whether taking one of those roads costs you anything in your main App Store search visibility.

## What Apple's own EU compliance page confirms

Apple's [DMA compliance page](https://developer.apple.com/support/dma-and-apps-in-the-eu/) lays out real, current rules for EU developers. Alternative app marketplaces are allowed. So is direct web distribution. That lets a user install your app straight from your own website. Apps installed this way need notarization. A real Apple review step, separate from normal App Store review. New business terms tied to all this start October 1, 2026. A specific, confirmed date on Apple's own page.

This is a real, documented shift. Not a vague policy gesture. EU developers have real, new choices to weigh right now. With a real deadline attached.

## Does it affect your App Store search ranking

Here's the part worth real care. Apple's own docs, as checked for this piece, don't say plainly that alternative distribution removes or demotes your app in normal App Store search. The two systems are documented apart. Marketplace and web rules on one page. Search and discovery on another. No direct line was found tying one to a penalty in the other.

That's not a guarantee nothing changes, though. Apple's own marketplace guidance covers real technical steps, like MarketplaceKit setup, that a blog post can't replace. If your business depends on this, confirm your exact case with Apple's own current docs. Not a summary of them.
