**Key points:**
- Neither major store publishes a confirmed day-of-week effect on review speed.
- A real mechanism, uneven submission volume across the week, could plausibly cause some variation, even without official confirmation.
- Developer folklore about "best days to submit" is common online, but it isn't documented store policy.
- Your own tracked submission-to-approval history is a better guide than a borrowed rule of thumb.

Ask around and you'll hear a confident answer about which day gets your app reviewed fastest. Check the real source behind that confidence, and there usually isn't one.

## What's actually known, and what isn't

Neither Apple nor Google states any day-of-week effect on review time. That's the honest starting point. The common "submit on a Tuesday, never a Friday" tip floating around online is folklore. Not real store policy. It might point at something true. Or it might just be a guess one person made once, that others copied without a check.

| | Said by either store |
|---|---|
| A specific best day to submit | No |
| Review time shifting with real queue volume | A fair guess, not a stated fact |

## Why there's still a real cause worth taking seriously

Review queues run on real volume. Not every app is likely sent in on the same day. If more teams submit right before a weekend, a queue could run a bit longer right after. That's a real, fair guess. It just hasn't been backed up by a real number from either store. Calling it settled fact goes further than what we actually know.

> **Real-world scenario:** A team had always avoided submitting on Fridays. They were following advice a developer had shared years earlier, with no real source behind it. Tracking their own submissions over several months showed no real difference between days at all. Their actual slowdown came from submission completeness, like missing metadata, not timing. Dropping the Friday rule and tightening their own checklist did far more for their real turnaround than any guess about the day ever had.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Don't treat day-of-week advice as confirmed fact.** Neither store has published a number behind it.
2. **Track your own submission time across several real submissions.** Your actual pattern beats anyone else's guess.
3. **Check your submission completeness first.** Missing metadata or unclear content causes far more real delay than timing does.
4. **Hold the idea loosely, not as a rule.** Volume might shift by day. That's different from knowing which day actually helps.
