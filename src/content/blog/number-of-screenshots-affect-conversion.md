**Key points:**
- The store sets a hard minimum and maximum. Inside that range, the number itself isn't what drives conversion.
- A strong set of four screenshots will usually beat a weak set of eight.
- A weak extra screenshot can dilute a strong early sequence instead of adding to it.
- The real question is whether you have that many genuinely different, useful things to show.

Most teams either upload every screenshot slot available, or stop as soon as they've filled the minimum. Neither habit is actually asking the right question.

## Why the count itself isn't the lever

Every screenshot you add costs a user's attention, even if it costs nothing to upload. A seventh screenshot that repeats your third one isn't neutral. It's attention a curious user spent on something that gave them nothing new. Right when they were deciding whether to install. More screenshots only helps if each one does real, distinct work. Otherwise you're just padding a sequence that was already strong enough to finish earlier.

| | What actually drives conversion |
|---|---|
| Hitting the maximum screenshot count | Nothing, on its own |
| Each screenshot showing something genuinely new | Real conversion signal |

## How to actually decide your real number

Start by listing the genuinely different things worth showing: the core feature, a second feature that matters, social proof, a key benefit. If that list has four real items, four screenshots beats eight where the last four just repeat or pad. If it has seven real items, use seven. The right count is whatever your actual list of distinct, useful things comes out to, not a target you picked first and then tried to fill.

> **Real-world scenario:** A team used all eight available screenshot slots, assuming more was simply safer than fewer. Their last three images reused angles already shown earlier in the sequence, just with different text. Testing a trimmed set of five, keeping only the screenshots that showed something genuinely new, improved conversion over the full set of eight, using less space to say more.

## What to actually do about it

1. **List the genuinely distinct things worth showing before picking a count.** Let that list set your number, not the store's maximum.
2. **Cut any screenshot that repeats an earlier one in a different outfit.** It isn't adding value, even if it isn't actively hurting anything either.
3. **Don't treat "more screenshots" as a safe default.** A weak extra image can dilute a strong early sequence.
4. **Test a trimmed set against your current one.** Fewer, stronger screenshots sometimes outperform a full set.
