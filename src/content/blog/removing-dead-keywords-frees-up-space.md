**Key points:**
- A dead keyword sits in a limited field without ever bringing real rank or visibility to your app.
- It isn't neutral. It's sitting on space a genuinely working term could use instead.
- Checking real rank data over a fair window is how you tell a truly dead keyword from one that just needs more time.
- Clearing out dead weight is usually worth more effort than hunting for a brand-new keyword to add on top.

A keyword field with a hard limit treats every term inside it as a real cost, whether that term is doing anything or not.

## Why a dead keyword isn't a neutral choice

Your keyword field has a fixed character limit. Every term inside it takes up real, limited space. A keyword that's never brought real rank or visibility isn't a harmless, quiet placeholder. It's actively using space a different, working term could use instead. Leaving it in isn't a safe choice. It's a choice that locks a better option out.

| | What it's actually doing |
|---|---|
| A working keyword | Earning its place with real rank or visibility |
| A dead keyword | Sitting in the same kind of space, producing nothing |

## Why it's easy to leave dead weight in place

Pulling a keyword feels like it needs a replacement ready to go, which can feel like more work than just leaving things as they are. That view skips a real win though. Clearing out something producing nothing has value on its own, even before you've picked exactly what fills the gap. An open slot in a tight field is worth having, rather than a slot quietly wasted on a term that was never working.

> **Real-world scenario:** A team had a keyword that had sat in their field for months with nothing to show for it, but kept it in place out of a vague worry they'd lose something by pulling it. Checking their real rank data, it had never brought anything meaningful in that whole stretch. Removing it and adding a new, more specific term in its place led to real, measurable visibility within a fair window, visibility that had been locked out the whole time by a term that was never doing its job.

## How to actually tell a dead keyword from a slow one

The real test is checking against actual rank data over a fair time window, not a gut feel for how long is "too long." A term with real relevance to your app that simply hasn't had enough time yet is a different case from one that's had a fair window and still shows nothing. Mixing up impatience with real deadness risks pulling something that just needed more time. Mixing up hope with real performance risks keeping something that was never going to work.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Check your keyword field against real rank data, not a hunch.** A term with no movement over a fair window is a real case for removal, not just a feeling.
2. **Treat a cleared slot as valuable on its own, even before you've found a replacement.** Freeing up space from dead weight is worth doing with no next term lined up yet.
3. **Don't mix up a slow keyword with a dead one.** Give genuinely relevant terms a fair window before calling them a failure.
4. **Revisit your full keyword field now and then, not just when adding something new.** Dead weight tends to build up quietly unless you make a point of checking for it.
