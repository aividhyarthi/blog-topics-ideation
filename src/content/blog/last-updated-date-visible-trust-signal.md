**Key points:**
- The visible update date is a different thing from the ranking signal. A human reads it directly. An algorithm processes the other.
- An old-looking date can make a user hesitant, suggesting the app was dropped even when it's actually fine.
- A fresh date usually reads as reassurance, though updates fired one right after another can sometimes read oddly.
- Treat this as its own job, separate from whatever the update-frequency ranking signal is doing behind the scenes.

Two different things key off the same event, an app update. Only one of them is something a human reader actually sees and judges.

## Why these are two separate signals

A ranking signal built around update frequency works inside the store's own system, feeding a calculation in whatever way that system weighs it. The visible "last updated" date is a whole different thing. It's text sitting right on your listing, read directly by a real person deciding whether to install. One lives inside an algorithm. The other lives in a human's own judgment, formed just by looking at a date.

| | Where it lives |
|---|---|
| Update-frequency ranking signal | Inside the store's own ranking math |
| Visible "last updated" date | Right in front of a human, judged on sight |

## Why an old date can cost you even if the app is fine

A user scanning a listing has no real way to know if an app is kept up, beyond whatever they can see. An old update date is one of the clearest signs they have, and it can read as a sign the app was dropped, whether or not that's true at all. The app being genuinely fine doesn't help if the one thing a user can actually see says otherwise.

> **Real-world scenario:** A team had a stable app that genuinely didn't need frequent updates, since nothing about it needed constant change to keep working well. Looking at their listing through a new user's eyes, the update date looked old enough to raise a real doubt about whether the app was still kept up at all. Pushing one small, low-risk update, just to refresh that date, with no real feature change needed, fixed a trust problem the app's actual stability had never caused in real life.

## Why this isn't the same as chasing the ranking signal

Managing the visible date is a separate job from managing the ranking signal, even though both come from the same act of shipping an update. A team chasing only the ranking signal might miss that the visible date is quietly working against them with real readers. A team fixing the visible date shouldn't assume that alone moves the ranking signal either.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Check how your listing's update date actually reads to a new user.** An old-looking date can raise doubt even when your app is genuinely fine.
2. **Treat the visible date and the ranking signal as two separate things.** One is read by humans directly. The other runs inside the store's own system.
3. **Consider a small, low-risk update just to refresh a stale-looking date.** This fixes trust, not necessarily a real feature need.
4. **Don't assume a very fresh date always helps.** A steady, reasonable pace tends to read better than either an old date or a burst of updates fired all at once.
