**Key points:**
- Apple's own guidance requires every Creative Asset to meet a 4+ standard. Even for an app rated 17+, or anything in between.
- A mature app's own key art might not qualify as-is. Apple's own example: a 17+ shooter can't use art showing gore or a weapon pointed at the viewer.
- Action imagery isn't banned outright. It just has to stay inside the 4+ content limits Apple's guidance describes.
- Apple told developers to start planning for this on August 5, 2026. Creative Assets themselves arrive this fall, alongside iOS 27 and iPadOS 27.

Your app might be rated 17+. Its Creative Assets still have to look like something a much younger audience could see. That's not a loophole. It's Apple's own stated rule.

## The rule, stated plainly

Apple's own guidance sets a 4+ bar for every Creative Asset. That's the rich image or video shown in the product page header and in search results. This holds no matter the app's own real age rating. A rating of 17+ for the app itself changes nothing about what its Creative Assets can show.

| App's own rating | Creative Asset content standard |
|---|---|
| 4+ | 4+ (no change) |
| 12+ | Still 4+ |
| 17+ | Still 4+ |

## What this actually rules out

Apple's own examples get specific. A shooter game rated 17+ can't use its own key art for a Creative Asset if that art shows gore, or a weapon pointed straight at a person or at the viewer. That's real, mature marketing art many studios already have on hand. Suddenly not usable in this one spot.

Action itself isn't off the table. Apple's own guidance says games with violence can still use action shots, as long as they stay within 4+ limits. The line sits at specific things. Graphic gore. A weapon aimed at the viewer. Not action or intensity in general. Apple's wider guidance also names other content to avoid across the board: explicit or harmful imagery, profanity, illegal drugs, self-harm, hate speech, stereotypes, sexual content, and inflammatory religious commentary.

> **Real-world scenario:** A studio behind a 17+ rated shooter planned to reuse their existing key art for their new Creative Asset. A close-up shot of a character aiming a weapon right at the camera. Reading Apple's own guidance on the 4+ rule, they realized that exact image wouldn't clear the bar. They commissioned a new piece showing the same character in an action pose, weapon lowered, keeping the intensity of the scene while staying inside the limits Apple described.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Don't assume your existing key art will transfer over.** A 17+ app's normal marketing art can fail a 4+ bar even if it's tame for that app's own genre.
2. **Start planning new assets now, not once the feature launches.** Apple gave developers notice back in August for exactly this reason. Real time to make art that fits the rule.
3. **Keep the action, drop the specific flagged content.** Apple's own guidance separates general intensity from specific banned elements like a weapon aimed at the viewer or graphic gore.
4. **Treat this as its own asset, not a repurposed one.** A Creative Asset may need its own dedicated production pass, separate from your existing screenshots and preview video.
