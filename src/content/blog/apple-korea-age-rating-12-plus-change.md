**Key points:**
- Apple's own August 2026 announcement confirms a Korea-specific change. Two content types move from All to 12+ starting October 2026.
- The two triggers: infrequent profanity and crude humor. And infrequent mature or suggestive themes.
- Nothing in your app's real content needs to change. This is a reclassification of existing content, not a new rule on content itself.
- Developers holding a GRAC classification number can override Apple's Korea rating directly. To All, 12+, 15+, or 19+.

An app that's shown an All rating in Korea for years might not stay there past October 2026. Nothing in the app changed. The classification did.

## What actually moved

Apple's own announcement names two specific content types. Both used to sit fine under an All rating in Korea. Starting October 2026, both push a listing to 12+ instead. Infrequent profanity and crude humor is one. Infrequent mature or suggestive themes is the other.

| Content type | Old Korea rating | New Korea rating |
|---|---|---|
| Infrequent profanity, crude humor | All | 12+ |
| Infrequent mature or suggestive themes | All | 12+ |

## Why this matters even for a mild app

A lot of apps carry a small bit of this kind of content without thinking of themselves as mature at all. A joke here. A suggestive line there. Nothing that reads as explicit. That's exactly what this change targets. A genuinely tame app can still cross from All to 12+ under the new mapping, just by touching one of these two categories at all, even rarely.

A 12+ rating can change who sees an app in search and browse. It can also affect whether it's eligible for certain spots aimed at younger users. For Korea, that's a real shift in visibility. Not just a label change.

## The override most developers miss

Apple's own update includes a real way out for developers who disagree with the automatic classification. A developer holding a GRAC classification number, Korea's own ratings body, can override Apple's Korea rating directly. All, 12+, 15+, or 19+. Set by the developer's own GRAC number, not Apple's content-based scoring alone.

> **Real-world scenario:** A casual puzzle game had carried an All rating in Korea since launch, with one joke line in its tutorial that technically counted as crude humor. After Apple's August 2026 announcement, the team checked their content against the new mapping. That single line would likely push them to 12+ once the change hit in October. They already held a GRAC classification from an earlier regional need. They used it to set the rating back to All directly in App Store Connect, skipping the automatic bump entirely.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Check your app for mild profanity or mild suggestive content before October 2026.** Even a small, easy-to-miss line can trigger the new 12+ mapping.
2. **Check whether you already hold a GRAC number.** If you do, Apple's own override lets you set your Korea rating directly instead of taking the automatic bump.
3. **Don't assume your content needs to change.** This is a rating mapping change. Not a new content rule. The fix might just be setting the right rating.
4. **Watch your Korea-specific numbers after October 2026.** A shift from All to 12+ is worth checking against your own install and search data for that market.
