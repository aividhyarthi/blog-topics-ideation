**Key points:**
- There's no real reason to expect a word already in your title gains extra weight from also sitting in your keyword field.
- A word is either relevant and indexed from where it sits. It doesn't compound by showing up more than once.
- In a field with a hard character limit, repeating a covered word is a real cost. It's space not spent on something new.
- Freeing up that space for a genuinely new term is usually the better use of the same limited room.

A word already doing its job in your title feels like it should get a reinforcing mention elsewhere too. The field it might go into doesn't work that way.

## Why repetition isn't reinforcement here

Your title and your keyword field are two separate places a word can be indexed from. A term in your title is already doing whatever work it's going to do there. Adding that same term to your keyword field doesn't stack a second layer on top. It's closer to a box that's already checked than a dial you can turn up by saying the same thing twice.

| | What repeating a word actually does |
|---|---|
| Expectation | A second mention adds extra ranking weight |
| What's more likely true | No extra weight. Just the same relevance already set once |

## Why the cost is real even if the benefit isn't

The keyword field has a hard character limit. Every character spent on a word your title already covers is a character you can't spend on a new term that field has never had room for. That's not a theory. It's a direct tradeoff between keeping a word that's already covered and adding one with no coverage anywhere on your listing at all.

> **Real-world scenario:** A team's keyword field had several words already sitting plainly in their app title, left over from an early draft nobody had touched since. Clearing those out freed up real space, enough to add two genuinely new, relevant terms the field had never had room for before. Their title's coverage of the removed words never changed at all. The new terms simply gave the listing coverage it didn't have, using space that repetition had been sitting on for no reason.

## What to actually check your field against

Before adding or keeping a word in your keyword field, check whether your title or subtitle already covers it. If it does, that word is a strong candidate for removal. Not because it's hurting anything, but because it's sitting on space a new term could use instead. The field works hardest when every word in it covers ground nothing else on your listing already covers.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Check your keyword field against your title and subtitle for overlap.** A word already covered there is a real candidate for removal.
2. **Don't assume repeating a word adds extra ranking weight.** There's no real reason to expect a second mention stacks on top of the first.
3. **Use the freed-up space for genuinely new, relevant terms.** That's where the field's tight space is more likely to actually help.
4. **Check your field now and then, not just when you first write it.** Old overlap tends to sit unnoticed until someone actually looks for it.
