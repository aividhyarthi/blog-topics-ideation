**Key points:**
- Google's own [app quality page](https://developer.android.com/quality) states directly that high-quality apps get rewarded with increased visibility on Google Play.
- It names four pillars: core value, user experience, technical quality, and privacy and security.
- Each pillar has a "Good" versus "Great" comparison, not just a pass or fail line.
- A separate, more detailed core quality guideline exists beyond these four high-level pillars.

Most teams treat "quality" as a vague, feel-good word. Google's own documentation treats it as something specific. Specific enough to name four parts and tie it straight to visibility.

## What Google's own page actually says

Google states plainly that high-quality apps get rewarded with more visibility on Google Play. That's not a vague pep talk. It's a direct claim. Quality itself ties to how much you get seen. The page then breaks "quality" into four named pillars, each with its own short line.

| Pillar | What Google says it covers |
|---|---|
| Core value | Being useful or fun |
| User experience | Being delightful to use |
| Technical quality | Making the most of premium devices |
| Privacy and security | Designing for safety |

## Why "Good" isn't the same bar as "Great"

Each pillar on Google's page has a "Good" bar and a "Great" one, side by side. Technical quality splits "works well on users' devices" from "makes the most of premium devices." That's a real gap. Meeting the bare minimum, and genuinely excelling, are two different outcomes here. Google's own page treats them as two distinct levels, not one pass-or-fail line.

> **Real-world scenario:** A team had treated "quality" internally as basically "no crashes." Their focus sat almost entirely on the technical side. Reading through Google's four-pillar breakdown surfaced two pillars, privacy and security plus core value, that their checklist had never really touched. Adding real checks for those two areas gave them a far more complete quality picture than their crash-only view ever had.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Check your own quality process against all four pillars, not just the technical one.** Crash rate is one part of one pillar. Not the whole picture.
2. **Aim for "Great," not just "Good," where it's realistic.** Google's own page treats these as two different bars, not one line to clear.
3. **Look at the more detailed core quality guideline too.** The four pillars are the high-level framing. A deeper checklist sits behind them.
4. **Remember visibility is the stated payoff.** Google frames this as a real lever, not just good practice for its own sake.
