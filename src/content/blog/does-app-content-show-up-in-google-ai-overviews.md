**Key points:**
- AI Overviews pull from Google's existing search index. No separate AI-only crawl exists. A page has to already be indexed, and ranking well, for a topic before it can be cited on it at all.
- One benchmark study of 1,259 real Google AI Overview citations found third-party list pages earned 63% of them. A separate analysis of nearly 400 million AI citations, by [Evertune](https://derivatex.agency/blog/what-content-gets-cited-google-ai-overviews/), found close to the same pattern.
- A different study, by Kevin Indig, looked at over 18,000 real ChatGPT citations. It found 44.2% pulled from a page's first 30% of text. That's a broad pattern across AI citation tools, not confirmed as specific to Google's AI Overviews.
- None of this is Google's own published rulebook. Treat it as strong outside research, not a confirmed official ranking factor.

Search results now often show an AI Overview before a single normal link. What gets quoted inside one isn't something Google has ever fully explained. But outside research gives a real, if partial, picture. And it's not great news for a lone app page hoping to get quoted directly.

## The one settled fact: no separate AI crawl

This part isn't in question. AI Overviews get built from Google's normal search index. The same one that powers plain blue-link results. There's no second, AI-only crawl feeding it. A page has to already be indexed. It also has to already rank fairly well for that topic. Only then does it stand a real chance of getting cited inside an Overview. Nothing new here beyond the same old SEO basics.

## What outside research shows about who gets cited

This is the part worth knowing. One study looked at 1,259 real Google AI Overview citations. Third-party list pages won 63% of them. Not single-product pages. Not brand homepages. Roundup and comparison content, written by someone other than the brand itself.

That number lines up with a second study too. [Evertune's](https://derivatex.agency/blog/what-content-gets-cited-google-ai-overviews/) own look at nearly 400 million AI citations found the same list-heavy pattern. Two separate studies landing near the same number is a real signal. Even without Google confirming its own method behind the scenes.

| What gets cited most | What the research shows |
|---|---|
| Third-party list or roundup pages | About 63% of citations, across two studies |
| Where in the page the quote comes from | 44.2% pull from the first 30% of the text (a broader pattern, not AI-Overview-specific) |
| A single app's own store page | Not confirmed by Google either way |

## Why the top of your page still matters

Kevin Indig looked at over 18,000 real ChatGPT citations. He found 44.2% came from a page's first 30% of text. That's a pattern seen across AI citation tools in general. It's not confirmed as unique to Google's AI Overviews. Still worth building around. If these systems lean toward text near the top, burying your best fact three screens down works against that pattern, not with it.

> **Real-world scenario:** A habit-tracking app's founder wanted their own app listing quoted inside an AI Overview for "best habit tracker apps." That never happened. But a comparison article on another site, one the founder had given a quote to, did get cited. Again and again, across several related searches. The real lesson wasn't that the app was invisible. It was that the citation system favored the roundup article over the brand's own page. Exactly the pattern the research points to.

## What to actually do with this

1. **Stop expecting your own store page to be the thing that gets quoted.** The research points toward outside roundup content instead.
2. **Get into the comparison articles that already rank well.** Real outreach, or a genuinely useful product, beats polishing your own listing copy alone.
3. **Put your clearest, most quotable facts near the top of anything you write.** That matches the pattern the research shows.
4. **Treat every number here as a strong clue, not an official rule.** None of it comes from Google's own published method.
