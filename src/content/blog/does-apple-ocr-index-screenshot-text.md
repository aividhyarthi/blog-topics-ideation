**Key points:**
- A claim that Apple reads screenshot text for ranking has spread widely. It traces back to one 2025 Appfigures post. Many ASO blogs now repeat it.
- No Apple page or forum post confirms this. The claim has never been checked against anything Apple itself said.
- The one real test, by ConsultMyApp and APPlyzer, checked 64 screenshot phrases across 8 top US apps. It found no strong evidence of broad indexing. Just one odd result.
- Screenshot captions still matter. For conversion. That's a separate, real effect from ranking.

A theory that started with one vendor's blog post has turned into something a lot of ASO content now repeats as settled fact. The real evidence doesn't back that up.

## Where the claim actually comes from

Per the sources repeating it, the root is a 2025 Appfigures analysis. It said Apple now reads some screenshot text and uses it to help rank apps. That's the real origin. Later posts mostly cite each other. Not Apple. A few even call it "confirmed." Without pointing to anything from Apple at all.

| Detail | What's actually true |
|---|---|
| Apple's own confirmation | None found |
| Origin of the claim | A 2025 third-party analysis |
| Rigorous test done | Yes, one: ConsultMyApp and APPlyzer |
| Test's result | No strong evidence of broad indexing |

## What the one real test actually found

ConsultMyApp and APPlyzer ran the closest thing to a real check. They tested 64 screenshot-derived phrases. Across 8 category-leading US apps. Their own conclusion: no strong evidence Apple broadly indexes screenshot titles. One odd result did show up, tied to Audible. But it could just as easily trace back to the keyword field, or something else entirely. Not screenshot text at all.

> **Real-world scenario:** A team spent a full sprint rewriting every screenshot caption, chasing a keyword they'd read was now "indexed from screenshots." Rankings for that term never moved. Reading the actual ConsultMyApp test later, they realized something. They'd never checked whether the claim had real evidence behind it. Not before spending real design time on it. They kept the new captions. Clearer copy still helped conversion. But they stopped treating screenshot text as a ranking lever going forward.

## Why this keeps spreading anyway

A claim that sounds plausible, gets repeated by enough sites, and never gets a clean Apple denial tends to turn into assumed fact. Nothing here is disproven either. The honest state is unresolved. One unverified claim at the start. One real test that found nothing solid. No primary source settling it either way. That's a different place to stand than "confirmed ranking factor." Which is how a lot of ASO content frames it today.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Don't redesign your screenshots around an unconfirmed theory.** Real keyword work belongs in title, subtitle, the keyword field, and description. Those fields are actually confirmed as indexed.
2. **Keep writing clear, keyword-aware captions anyway.** That helps conversion. A real, separate effect from ranking. Worth doing regardless.
3. **Check any ASO claim for its real source first.** Ten blogs citing the same one post isn't ten confirmations.
4. **Watch for an actual Apple statement.** Not another blog repeating the same 2025 claim. Until one shows up, treat this as unresolved.
