**Key points:**
- Changing your primary language doesn't reset your existing keyword rankings in markets you're already active in.
- Your ratings, reviews, and install history all stay with your app, no matter which language is marked primary.
- The real change lands on markets with no listing of their own. Those switch to your new default language instead.
- Switching your primary language is a different move from adding a local listing, even though both touch language.

A setting called "primary" sounds like it should carry real weight if you flip it. The real effect is smaller than that name suggests.

## What actually changes

Your primary language is a fallback. It's the text shown in any market where you haven't built your own separate listing. Flip it, and that fallback text changes. What doesn't change is any market that already has its own listing. Those keep showing their own text, no matter what your primary language is set to. Your keyword rankings in active markets don't reset either. Neither do your ratings, reviews, or install history. All of that stays with your app no matter what.

| | What happens when you switch primary language |
|---|---|
| Markets with their own listing | No change, they keep their own text |
| Markets with no listing of their own | Now show your new default language instead |
| Existing keyword rankings | Not reset by this change alone |
| Ratings, reviews, install history | Stay with your app either way |

## Why it's easy to assume a bigger effect

The word "primary" sounds like it should touch everything underneath it, the way a primary key touches every row in a table. App listings don't work that way. Each market has its own text, and a market's own listing always wins over your primary language default. The primary setting only fills the gap where no local listing exists. That's a much smaller reach than the name alone suggests.

> **Real-world scenario:** A team switched their app's primary language ahead of a push into a new region. They expected the change to shift how their app showed up everywhere. Checking their old markets after, every one with its own listing looked exactly the same as before. Only a few small markets, ones that had never had a listing of their own, switched to the new default language. Their rankings in the big, old markets hadn't moved at all. The change had landed exactly where it should have. Just on a much smaller slice of their app than the team first thought.

## What this means for planning a change

If you're weighing a primary language switch, ask one simple question first. Which of your markets lack a listing of their own right now? Those are the only ones that will actually change. Markets with their own listing stay untouched, and your wider ranking and review history travels with your app no matter what. Treating this as a small, narrow setting, not a sweeping one, matches what it actually does.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Check which markets lack a listing of their own before you switch.** That's the one group that will actually show new text afterward.
2. **Don't expect your keyword rankings to move from this change alone.** A market with its own listing keeps that listing no matter what your primary language is.
3. **Treat adding a local listing and switching primary language as two separate moves.** One builds a real page for one market. The other just changes your fallback text.
4. **Confirm your history stayed intact after the switch.** Your ratings, reviews, and install counts aren't touched by this setting, and a quick check can confirm nothing else moved either.
