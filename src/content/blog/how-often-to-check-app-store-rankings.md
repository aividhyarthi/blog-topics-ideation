There are two different questions hiding inside "how's my app ranking," and most people only ever check one of them: **where do my handful of core keywords stand today**, and **how big and how well-covered is my whole keyword universe**. They need different check frequencies, and treating them the same is the most common reason rank data ends up either stale or misleading.

## The two clocks

**Daily-tracked keywords** (your core 10, 30, or 60 terms, whatever your plan allows) are cheap enough to check every single day. This is the number you watch for week-to-week movement: did that title change three days ago actually move the needle, or was it noise?

**Coverage keywords** (the full universe, potentially hundreds or thousands of terms including long-tail and exploratory ones) are too expensive to search live on every page load. These get checked by a background job that works through the list over whatever time it has, and a big list can take more than one run to finish a full pass.

Confusing the two clocks is where the frustration usually starts: opening a 600-keyword coverage list and seeing half of it marked "not checked yet" isn't a bug, it's the natural state of a list that large partway through its check cycle. The fix is patience (or a smaller list), not refreshing the page.

| List type | Check frequency | What it answers |
|---|---|---|
| Daily-tracked (core keywords) | Every day, automatically | "Did my last change move rank on the terms I care about most?" |
| Coverage (full universe) | Background, resumable. Can span multiple runs on large lists | "What's my realistic reach across every term worth knowing about, even ones I'm not actively optimizing for?" |

## Why daily-only tracking misses the bigger picture

If you only ever watch your 20 core keywords, you'll catch tactical wins and losses but miss the strategic picture: is your keyword *universe* itself growing? Are there terms you're accidentally ranking well for that you haven't noticed and could double down on? A coverage check answers questions a narrow daily list structurally can't, because it was never asked those questions in the first place.

> **Real-world scenario:** A finance app's daily-tracked list of 25 core terms looked flat for six weeks, no meaningful movement up or down, which read as "ASO work isn't working." Its 400-term coverage list, checked in the background over the same period, told a different story: it had picked up top-30 rankings on 40+ long-tail terms nobody had deliberately targeted, almost all variants of "SIP calculator" and "mutual fund tracker." The daily list was genuinely flat. The actual visibility trend, invisible without the wider list, was clearly upward, and pointed straight at which two terms deserved promotion into the daily-tracked set.

## What "checking" should actually mean in practice

A useful weekly routine, once both clocks are running:

1. **Glance at the daily trend** for your core terms. Is the direction (not the single-day number) up, flat, or down over the trailing week?
2. **Skim the coverage list's newly-ranking terms**: anything climbing that wasn't there last month is either a signal your broader content/listing work is landing, or an opportunity to promote a term into your actively-optimized set.
3. **Check the coverage list's actual size against last month**: [a keyword universe that isn't growing](/rank) usually means research has stalled, not that there's nothing left to find.
4. Resist the urge to react to any single day's number on either list. The trend over multiple checks is the signal, one reading almost never is.

The mistake isn't checking too often or too rarely in the abstract. It's running only one clock and expecting it to answer both questions.
