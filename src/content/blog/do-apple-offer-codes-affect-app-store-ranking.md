**Key points:**
- Apple's [subscriptions page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/) calls offer codes a real tool for new signups, keeping users, and winning back people who churned. You choose how to hand them out.
- Apple's [own rules page](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-in-app-purchases/create-offer-codes-for-in-app-purchases) sets hard limits. Up to 10 active offers per app. Up to 1 million codes per app, per quarter.
- That same page says nothing about redemption data or a rank effect. A real, notable gap.
- A [real 2020 forum thread](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/667675) shows this exact mix-up isn't new. Developers have asked this for years.

Offer codes get used all the time. Promos, press outreach, win-back deals. Whether a redemption counts toward rank at all is a real, honest unknown. Apple writes a lot about the mechanics here. It says nothing about this one specific question.

## What Apple actually says about offer codes

Apple's [subscriptions page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/) lists real, confirmed uses. New signups. Keeping current users. Winning back people who left. You pick your own way to give codes out. A user redeems one directly. No normal App Store purchase step needed for that one action. A real, useful, well-documented tool.

Apple's [own page on setting up offer codes](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-in-app-purchases/create-offer-codes-for-in-app-purchases) adds real, hard limits on top. Up to 10 active offers per app at once. Up to 1 million codes per app, each quarter. Real numbers, straight from Apple. Not a guess passed around online.

## What Apple stays quiet on

Here's the honest gap. Neither page found says how a redeemed code gets counted for rank. No mention of redemption data anywhere. No stated link to download counts or rank signals either way. A real, clear silence inside otherwise detailed pages. Worth naming plainly instead of guessing at an answer Apple hasn't given.

## Why the honest unknown still matters

A [real 2020 forum thread](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/667675) shows a developer confused about this exact point. What a redeemed code actually counts as, beyond the plain revenue and retention win. That confusion clearly hasn't fully cleared up years later. No page found closes the gap. Treat offer codes as a real, confirmed tool for growth and keeping users. Treat any specific rank claim tied to them as a guess. Not something Apple has stated.

> **Real-world scenario:** A language app ran an offer code deal with a podcast. Listeners got a free month of premium features. The deal drove a real, clear spike in new sign-ups during that window. The team hoped it would lift their category rank too. They assumed redemptions counted like a normal download. Their rank held roughly flat through the whole campaign. Their user count and revenue, the two things Apple's own pages actually promise here, both moved the right way. A useful deal. Just not for the reason the team first expected.

## What to actually do with offer codes

1. **Use offer codes for what Apple actually promises.** New users, keeping current ones, and win-back deals. Real, confirmed wins.
2. **Don't count on a rank lift from an offer code push.** No page backs that specific hope.
3. **Track your own user count and revenue around each deal.** That's the real effect Apple documents here.
4. **Watch your rank too, out of plain curiosity.** If it moves, that's real data Apple itself hasn't published.
