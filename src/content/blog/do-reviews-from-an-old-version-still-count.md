**Key points:**
- A review's star rating stays counted in your average forever, no matter how old the app version it was written about.
- Being outdated isn't a removal reason on its own. Stores remove reviews for policy violations, not for age.
- A public reply can give new readers context on an old complaint, even though it won't change the star math.
- Some users do go back and update old reviews once they notice a fix, but that only happens if they choose to.

That one-star review about a crash from eighteen months ago is still doing exactly what it did the day it was posted.

## Why age doesn't remove a review's weight

Once a review is posted, it counts toward your rating average at the star value it was given, full stop. The store doesn't track which version of your app the reviewer had installed, and it doesn't quietly decay a review's weight as time passes. A bug fixed two years ago and a bug that's still live today look identical in the math, as long as the review describing either one is still sitting there unedited.

| | Still counts toward your average? |
|---|---|
| A review from an old, since-fixed bug | Yes, at its original star value |
| A review from today | Yes, at its current star value |

## Why "it's outdated" isn't a removal argument

Both major stores remove reviews for policy violations: spam, harassment, fake reviews, that kind of thing. Being out of date isn't one of those categories. A review complaining about a problem you genuinely fixed a year ago is still a real review of a real experience the user actually had, even if it no longer reflects your app today. That's frustrating, but it's not grounds for removal on its own.

> **Real-world scenario:** A team fixed a crash bug that had generated a cluster of one-star reviews, and assumed those reviews would eventually stop counting once the fix shipped. Months later, the same old reviews were still sitting on the listing at full weight, quietly holding the average down even though the actual bug was long gone. Replying publicly to the most visible of them, noting the fix and the version it shipped in, didn't change the star math, but it gave anyone reading the listing today the context the raw number didn't offer.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Don't expect an old review to fade on its own.** It counts at full weight for as long as it stays posted and unedited.
2. **Reply publicly to outdated complaints you've genuinely fixed.** It won't move your rating, but it gives new readers real context.
3. **Don't request removal just because a review is old.** Stores act on policy violations, not on age, however unfair that feels.
4. **Keep fixing the underlying issues anyway.** New reviews, at your current real quality, are the only thing that actually moves the average forward.
