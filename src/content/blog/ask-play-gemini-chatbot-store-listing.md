**Key points:**
- Ask Play is a Gemini-powered chat feature inside Google Play, [announced at Google I/O 2026](https://9to5google.com/2026/05/19/google-play-store-gets-new-ask-play-chatbot-as-gemini-starts-recommending-apps/) and confirmed by [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-play-connected-app-for-gemini-rollout-3681969/).
- It's built for "deeper search journeys," helping users find an app through conversation instead of a keyword search box.
- It ships with a "Highlights" feature that shows summarized information at the top of results.
- Gemini is also being connected more broadly, letting people discover, install, and even make in-app purchases through a chat interface outside the Play Store app itself.

Google Play's search box has had one job for over a decade. Match keywords to listings. Ask Play changes that job. It's a chat interface. It's built for people who don't know exactly what they're looking for yet.

## What Ask Play actually is

Ask Play is a Gemini-powered overlay inside Google Play. Google built it for what it calls "deeper search journeys." Instead of typing "meditation app," a user can describe what they want in a sentence. They get a conversational answer back. It launched with a "Highlights" feature too. That pulls a short summary to the top of results, ahead of the usual ranked list.

This isn't just a Play Store feature either. Gemini is getting wired into Android more broadly. A connected Google Play app for Gemini is rolling out now. It lets people discover apps, install them, and make in-app purchases, all from inside a chat. Not the Play Store app itself.

| Feature | What it does |
|---|---|
| Ask Play | Conversational app search and recommendations inside Google Play |
| Highlights | Summarized info surfaced at the top of a search, ahead of the ranked list |
| Gemini-connected Play app | Discover, install, and buy in-app, from a chat interface outside Play itself |

## Why this changes what your listing needs to say

A keyword search just matches words. A chatbot has to actually understand what your app does. Well enough to describe it back to someone in a plain sentence. That's a much higher bar. Vague, keyword-stuffed copy can still rank fine in plain search. But it reads as empty or confusing to something trying to summarize your app for a real question.

Some outside ASO analysis reports that Ask Play draws from both your Play Store description and your app's own website. Google hasn't published every source it pulls from in a technical document. So treat that specific detail as reported, not officially confirmed line by line. But the basic logic still holds. If a system is trying to represent your app accurately in one sentence, any place your own copy contradicts itself works against you.

> **Real-world scenario:** A recipe app's Play Store description called it "the fastest way to plan your week," while its own marketing site pitched it as "a grocery list app that also does recipes." Neither was wrong exactly, but they described two different products. After a support ticket mentioned a user got confused by an AI-generated answer that mixed both framings into something inaccurate, the team rewrote both to lead with the same core description. The inconsistency, not either individual page, was the actual problem.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Say the same thing about your app everywhere.** Your Play Store description and your own website should describe the same core value, in compatible language, not necessarily identical words.
2. **Write one clear sentence that answers "what does this app do."** That's the sentence most likely to get pulled into a conversational answer or a Highlights summary.
3. **Don't treat every claim here as Google's official rulebook.** The chat feature and Highlights are confirmed. The exact list of sources it reads from is reported, not published by Google.
