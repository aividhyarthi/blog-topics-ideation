**Key points:**
- AppFollow's own [2026 benchmark report](https://appfollow.io/blog/app-review-reply-rate-benchmarks-2026) covers 92.9 million reviews across roughly 20,700 apps, on both the App Store and Google Play, from July 2025 to July 2026.
- The market-wide reply rate sits at 24.2%. Finance tops the list at 51%, the only industry above half. Social sits lowest, at 9%.
- Reply speed ranges from 43 hours in Finance to 204 hours in Education, per the same report.
- AppFollow is a review-management vendor. This is its own telemetry, not an independent or academic study. Treat the numbers as a vendor benchmark, not a verified industry fact.

Most apps still don't reply to most of their reviews. A fresh, large-sample report puts a real number on exactly how uneven that habit is.

## What the report actually measured

Per AppFollow's own report, the dataset spans 92.9 million reviews. About 20,700 apps. Both major stores. A full year, July 2025 to July 2026, split across 13 industries. That's a real, dated sample. Not a handful of apps or a short window.

| Detail | What the report states |
|---|---|
| Market-wide reply rate | 24.2% |
| Highest-replying industry | Finance, at 51% |
| Lowest-replying industry | Social, at 9% |
| Fastest reply speed | Finance, 43 hours |
| Slowest reply speed | Education, 204 hours |

## Where the gap actually sits

Finance stands alone above the halfway mark. Every other industry replies to less than half its reviews. Social sits at the opposite end. Under one in ten get a reply. The report also tracks a "reply effect," its own term for how reply rate tracks with rating movement. Health & Sports shows the strongest positive effect, at +1.39 points. Education and Social are the two industries where that effect runs slightly negative instead.

The report also flags its own paying customers separately. Apps using AppFollow's reply tools average around 41%. That's well above the 24.2% market figure. The gap makes sense. A company that pays for review-management tooling has already decided replying matters. The comparison isn't neutral. The market-wide figure is the more honest one for most teams to measure against.

> **Real-world scenario:** A support lead at a mid-size fintech app assumed their reply habits were about average, since nobody on the team had ever benchmarked it. Reading this report, they found their actual numbers put them closer to the Finance-wide 51% than to the 24.2% market average, mostly because a compliance requirement already had someone reading every 1 and 2-star review daily. The report gave them something they didn't have before: a real number to tell leadership, instead of a guess about whether their reply habit was actually good or just felt that way.

## What the report stops short of proving

Apps replying to 30-50% of reviews carry a 3.77 average rating. The report states that as a correlation, not a causal claim. Apps that reply more may also just run better support. Or have fewer real bugs. Or sit in categories where ratings run higher anyway. The report doesn't separate those explanations. Treat the number as "worth tracking." Not "guaranteed if you reply more."

## What to actually do with this

1. **Pull your own reply rate before comparing it to anything.** Most teams genuinely don't know their own number until they check.
2. **Compare against your actual industry, not the flat 24.2% market figure.** A finance app benchmarked against Social's 9% looks great for the wrong reason.
3. **Prioritize speed on your lowest-rated reviews first.** The report's own fastest industry, Finance, is also its highest-replying one. Those two numbers likely reinforce each other.
4. **Treat this as one vendor's dataset, not a verified industry standard.** Useful for a rough benchmark. Not something to cite as independently audited fact.
