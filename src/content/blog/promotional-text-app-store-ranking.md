**Key points:**
- Promotional text is a short field on your App Store listing that you can edit any time, without a new app version.
- It isn't a search-indexed field. It won't help your keyword coverage the way your title or subtitle field does.
- It's shown to users browsing your listing, sitting above your full description.
- Its real value is speed: changing a timely message without waiting on a release cycle.

A text field you can update without submitting a new build sounds like it should matter for search. It doesn't, and that's by design.

## What promotional text actually is

It's a short block of text that sits at the top of your App Store description, visible to anyone browsing your listing. The distinct thing about it isn't where it sits. It's that you can change it on a live app at any time, without App Review, without a new build, without the wait a normal update carries. Nothing else on your listing works quite that way. Your title, subtitle, and screenshots are all tied to a version submission. Promotional text stands apart from that cycle entirely.

| Field | Editable without a new version? | Searchable? |
|---|---|---|
| Promotional text | Yes | No |
| Title / subtitle | No, tied to a version | Yes |
| Keyword field | No, tied to a version | Yes (not shown to users) |

## Why it's easy to mistake for a ranking lever

A field you can freely edit feels like it should be worth optimizing the way a keyword field is. It isn't built for that job. It's not part of either store's search index, so stuffing it with keyword variations does nothing for your visibility in search. What it's actually built for is speed of messaging, telling users something timely without waiting on the days a normal review cycle can take. Treating it as a hidden keyword slot is a reasonable guess that just doesn't match what the field is for.

> **Real-world scenario:** A team wanted to highlight a seasonal discount that would only run for two weeks. Submitting a new version with the message baked into the description would have meant review time eating into the promotion window, maybe losing several of the fourteen days before the message was even live. They updated the promotional text field instead. It went live immediately, no review wait at all. When the promotion ended, they swapped the text out again just as fast. None of it touched their search ranking either way, since the field was never part of that system to begin with.

## What it's genuinely good for

Short-lived, timely messages are where this field earns its place. A seasonal offer. A note about a feature that just shipped. A quick heads-up tied to a specific event or window. Anything where waiting on a full release cycle would mean missing the moment the message actually matters. That's a real, practical use. It's just a different job than the one search-indexed fields do.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Use promotional text for timely messages, not keyword coverage.** It isn't indexed, so keyword-stuffing it accomplishes nothing for search.
2. **Take advantage of the fast edit cycle for anything time-sensitive.** A seasonal note or a quick update about a new feature is exactly what this field is built for.
3. **Keep your actual keyword work in the fields that are indexed.** Title, subtitle, and the keyword field are where that effort belongs.
4. **Don't expect a ranking change from editing it.** Whatever moves your rank, it isn't this field, by design.
