**Key points:**
- Google's own March 2026 announcement describes a new, dedicated PC section inside the Games tab. It gives cross-platform titles high-visibility placement among active mobile players.
- A PC badge recognizes a title's cross-platform work, per Google's own wording, and creates more chances to move mobile players toward a PC version.
- Buy once, play anywhere lets a single purchase unlock both mobile and PC versions for select paid titles. A related but separate feature.
- This is a real, added discovery surface for cross-platform games. Not a swap for normal keyword and category visibility.

A game's mobile and PC versions used to live in separate worlds, as far as discovery goes. Google's own March 2026 announcement changes that with a surface built to link the two.

## What Google's own announcement describes

The Games tab now has a dedicated PC section. Per Google's own wording, it gives PC titles high-visibility placement among people already active on mobile. That's a real, added channel. Separate from normal search and category browsing. It targets players already using Google Play Games. Many don't know a title they play on mobile also has a PC version.

| Feature | What it does |
|---|---|
| PC section in Games tab | Shows PC titles to active mobile players directly |
| PC badge | Signals cross-platform work to browsing users |
| Buy once, play anywhere | One purchase unlocks both mobile and PC versions |

## Why the badge matters beyond a visual label

The PC badge isn't just decoration. Google's own announcement frames it as real recognition of cross-platform work. The kind of signal that can shape a browsing player's choice when comparing titles. For a studio that's built and kept up both a mobile and PC version, the badge makes that work visible right where players are deciding what to try next.

Buy once, play anywhere adds a real reason to act on top of visibility. For a paid title, one purchase covering both platforms removes a real sticking point. No one likes paying twice for what feels like the same game. That pairs well with the new PC section. Visibility alone doesn't convert if the price puts people off trying the PC version too.

> **Real-world scenario:** A studio had kept up both a mobile and PC version of their game for over a year, with almost no overlap between the two player bases. Most mobile players had no idea a PC version even existed. After Google's PC section and badge rolled out, their title started showing up in that new section to their own mobile players. They also switched new purchases to buy once, play anywhere pricing. Within a few months, a real share of their PC installs came straight from existing mobile players finding the badge, instead of separate PC-only marketing.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Check whether your cross-platform title is actually showing the PC badge.** If you keep up both versions, confirm your Play Console setup actually reflects that pairing.
2. **Consider buy once, play anywhere if you sell a paid title on both platforms.** It removes a real buying hurdle right when the new PC section is sending you more cross-platform attention.
3. **Don't treat the PC section as a swap for normal ASO work.** It's an added surface for players already in the Google Play Games world. Not a stand-in for keyword and category visibility.
4. **Track whether PC installs start shifting toward your own mobile audience.** That's the real sign this feature is working, not just impressions on the new surface.
