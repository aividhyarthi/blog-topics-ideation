**Key points:**
- Apple's own [App Store Connect guidance](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-in-app-purchases/set-a-price-for-an-in-app-purchase) confirms a single base price generates prices across "the other 174 storefronts and 43 currencies."
- You get up to 800 price points by default, with a request process available for 100 more, up to $10,000.
- Apple's own words guarantee your base country's price never moves on its own, and you always get advance notice before any other storefront's price changes.
- Manually overriding a price anywhere removes that storefront from automatic updates. Per Apple's own wording, you become responsible for keeping it current yourself.

One decision, your base country and base price, quietly sets prices in 174 other places. Without you touching a thing.

## What Apple's own guidance actually confirms

Per Apple's own words, you "choose a base country or region." Apple uses it "to provide comparable prices on the other 174 storefronts." That's the real mechanic here. Not 174 separate choices. One base. Spread out automatically. Following "the most common pricing convention for each country or region."

| Detail | What Apple's own guidance confirms |
|---|---|
| Price points available | Up to 800 by default, 100 more by request |
| Highest reachable price | Up to $10,000 |
| Storefronts covered by one base | The other 174, across 43 currencies |
| Base country price | Never changed by Apple without your action |

## Why the base country choice actually matters

Apple's own page keeps this simple. The base is just "the country or region you're familiar with." No real guidance beyond that. That's a real gap worth filling in yourself. Your base isn't just a starting point. It's the anchor every other price gets measured against, as rates and taxes shift over time. Pick it just because it's your home market, without checking your real biggest audience, and every future update traces back to a reference point that may not match where your real money comes from.

> **Real-world scenario:** A productivity app set its purchase base in a small home market. Mostly out of habit. Most of their real paying users lived elsewhere. In markets with very different currency swings. Reading Apple's own pricing page, the team saw it clearly. Their biggest markets were tracking a base with little to do with their real revenue. They switched the base to their largest actual market instead. Future updates then tracked the currency and tax shifts that actually mattered to their money.

## What manually overriding a price actually costs you

Apple's own words are blunt about the tradeoff. "If you select your own prices, Apple won't adjust your pricing on those storefronts in the future." Not a small detail. Every storefront you set by hand becomes your own job. For currency moves and tax changes both, going forward. A one-time fix, to hit a round number or a familiar local price, can quietly turn into a task nobody remembers a year later.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Pick your base country based on real revenue.** Not just the market you happen to know best.
2. **Let automatic pricing handle most storefronts.** Apple's own system already tracks local convention. And tax and rate shifts.
3. **Save manual overrides for a real, specific reason.** Each one becomes a standing job. Not a one-time choice.
4. **Revisit your base if your real user base has shifted since launch.** The anchor for every other price should match where your business is now.
