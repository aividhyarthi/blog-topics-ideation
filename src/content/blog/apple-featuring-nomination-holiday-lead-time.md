**Key points:**
- Apple's own [App Store Connect Help page](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-featuring-nominations/nominate-your-app-for-featuring) states a minimum lead time of 3 weeks for a featuring nomination. Not the 8-12 weeks some third-party guides claim.
- Three nomination types exist: New Content, App Enhancements, and App Launch, each needing a clear, specific description of the update.
- You can attach an approved or published In-App Event, plus up to 5 supporting URLs, including TestFlight public links.
- Submission works two ways: one at a time through App Store Connect, or in bulk through a CSV import. CSV submissions go in automatically and can't be saved as a draft first.

Holiday planning season means a lot of guessing about how early to ask Apple for a feature slot. Apple's own page actually names a number.

## What Apple's own page actually confirms

Per Apple's own words: "To ensure operational efficiency, we recommend submitting plans as early as possible, with a minimum lead time of 3 weeks." That's a real, stated floor, not a vague suggestion pieced together from blog posts.

| Detail | What Apple's own page confirms |
|---|---|
| Minimum lead time | 3 weeks |
| Who can submit | Account Holder, Admin, App Manager, or Marketing role |
| Nomination types | New Content, App Enhancements, App Launch |
| Supporting materials | An In-App Event, plus up to 5 URLs |

## Why the "3 weeks" number actually matters

Third-party ASO guides have floated very different numbers here. Some say 8 to 12 weeks. Others say 3. That gap is wide. Wide enough to change a real holiday plan. One team might start a nomination in September for a December feature. Another might wait until late November, thinking they still have time. Apple's own page settles this. 3 weeks is the floor it names. In its own words. Not a vendor's guess.

> **Real-world scenario:** A shopping app wanted a feature slot timed to a big December sale. The marketing lead had read a guide online claiming 10 weeks of lead time, and nearly delayed the whole campaign waiting to "be safe" before submitting. Checking Apple's own App Store Connect Help page directly, they found the real stated minimum was 3 weeks. They still submitted early, in case review or editorial review took longer than the floor, but they stopped treating a vendor's guess as a hard deadline that didn't actually exist in Apple's own text.

## The three nomination types, and what each actually needs

Apple's own page splits nominations into three types. New Content covers new offers, content, or events inside an app. App Enhancements covers new features or big updates. App Launch covers a brand-new app or pre-order. For the first two, Apple's own guidance asks for three things. A clear statement of purpose and priority. Specific details about the update. The real goal of the new feature. Not a generic pitch.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Treat 3 weeks as the real floor, not the real target.** Apple's own page recommends submitting "as early as possible." The 3-week number is a minimum, not a comfortable margin.
2. **Pick the right nomination type before writing anything else.** New Content, App Enhancements, and App Launch each ask for different specifics.
3. **Attach a live or approved In-App Event early if you have one.** Apple's own page recommends attaching it "as early as possible," separately from the nomination itself.
4. **Use the CSV path only when you're confident in the details.** It submits automatically, with no draft stage to catch a mistake before it goes in.
