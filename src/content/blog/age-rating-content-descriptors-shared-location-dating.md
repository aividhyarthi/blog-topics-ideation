**Key points:**
- Apple added two new age rating content descriptors: Shared Location and Dating and Relationships, starting [November 16, 2026](https://developer.apple.com/app-store-connect/whats-new/).
- Every app must complete these new declarations by January 12, 2027, per Apple's own stated deadline.
- The rule applies to any app with these features, not only apps built specifically around them.
- This sits in the age rating questionnaire, the same place that already asks about other content types.

Apple just added two new boxes to check on the age rating form. And gave every app a real date to check them by.

## What the two new descriptors actually cover

Apple's own page names them: Shared Location, and Dating and Relationships. Both joined the age rating form in App Store Connect starting November 16, 2026. They sit next to the other content categories the form already asks about. Not a separate new form. If your app genuinely has either kind of feature, this is where you now say so.

| | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Shared Location | A feature that shares a user's location with others |
| Dating and Relationships | A feature built around dating or relationship-focused interaction |

## Why this isn't just for dating apps

The rule reaches further than apps built around these features as their main point. A fitness app with an optional "share my run live" feature has a real Shared Location feature. Even though sharing a run isn't the app's core pitch. A general social app with a dating mode tucked in settings has a real Dating and Relationships feature too. Apple's deadline doesn't skip apps where the feature is just a small part of the whole.

> **Real-world scenario:** A team running a general social app had added an optional "find nearby matches" mode months earlier. They treated it as a minor feature, not a defining one. When the new form fields appeared, they first assumed the rule only applied to apps built specifically around dating. Reading Apple's own description cleared that up fast. Any app with the feature, big or small, had to answer honestly. They updated their form ahead of the deadline instead of risking a problem found later.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Check your app for either feature, even a small or optional one.** The rule covers the feature's presence, not how central it is to your app.
2. **Mark January 12, 2027 as a real deadline.** Apple's own page states it plainly, not as a soft suggestion.
3. **Update your form directly in App Store Connect.** It sits with the existing content-rating questions, not a separate process.
4. **Check this again when you add a new feature later.** A location or dating-adjacent feature added after today still needs the same honest answer.
