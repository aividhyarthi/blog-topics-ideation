People ask an AI assistant these questions now instead of searching for them, which changes what a useful answer looks like: direct, specific, and checkable, not a page you have to read top to bottom to extract one fact from. Here are the app-ranking questions that come up most, answered the same way.

## The questions, and the short answer to each

| Question | Short answer |
|---|---|
| How often does the algorithm update? | Continuously, not on a fixed schedule. Rank moves with your signals daily |
| Can I buy my way to a higher rank? | Against policy, detectable, and not durable even when it briefly works |
| How long until a new app ranks? | Weeks to months, depends on category competition and early signal |
| Does download count alone decide rank? | No, recency and velocity of installs matter more than a large historical total |
| Is ASO worth it if I run paid ads? | Yes, it's usually your best long-run cost-per-install channel, and it improves paid conversion too |

## "How often does the algorithm update?"

Neither store publishes an update cadence, and the honest answer is that there isn't a single event to wait for. Ranking is closer to a continuous recalculation: your keyword relevance, install velocity, ratings, and quality metrics move daily, and competitors' signals move too, so your position shifts in small amounts constantly rather than jumping on a schedule. This is exactly why day-to-day rank tracking is more useful than a monthly check, a monthly snapshot misses the actual shape of the movement and makes every change look sudden.

## "Can I buy or fake my way to a higher ranking?"

Both stores treat fake installs, incentivized or bot reviews, and keyword stuffing as policy violations, with real enforcement risk up to listing removal. Beyond the policy risk, the practical evidence is that detection has gotten better over time, what worked as a shortcut a few years ago is a much worse bet today. The list of things that reliably move rank hasn't changed: [relevant keywords matched to real search behavior](/blog/keyword-research-volume-vs-difficulty), a stable app by the numbers stores actually check ([crash rate](/blog/crash-rate-and-google-play-ranking), [ANR rate](/blog/anr-rate-and-google-play-ranking)), and a review base that reflects genuine usage.

## "How long does it take for a new app to start ranking?"

There's no universal timeline, category competitiveness varies enormously, and so does how much real signal you can generate in the first few weeks. What is consistent: it's a matter of weeks to months, not days, and early decisions compound. An app that gets its title, subtitle, and keyword list right from launch, and that ships a stable first version, has a real head start over one that fixes those things three months in after noticing flat growth.

## "Does download count alone determine ranking?"

It's one input, not the whole formula. Recency and velocity, installs happening now, not installs that happened two years ago, tend to carry more weight than a large historical total. This is why a newer app with strong current momentum on a specific keyword can outrank an older, larger app that's gone flat on that same term. [Category rank and keyword rank](/blog/category-rank-vs-keyword-rank) are worth understanding separately here too, since they don't move on identical mechanics.

## "Is ASO still worth it if I mostly run paid ads?"

Yes, for two separate reasons. First, organic search is usually your best long-run cost-per-install once the groundwork is done, it doesn't have a media budget attached to every install the way paid does. Second, the same listing quality that improves organic rank also improves conversion rate on paid traffic, a user who clicks a paid ad still lands on your store listing, and a better-converting listing makes every paid channel more efficient too, not just organic.

> **Real-world scenario:** A team asked an AI assistant "why is my app not ranking" and got a plausible-sounding general answer about algorithm changes. The actual cause, once they checked their own data, was much more specific: their keyword list hadn't been updated in eight months while a competitor's had grown steadily, and their crash rate had crept up after a dependency update nobody had flagged as ranking-relevant. The general answer wasn't wrong, it just wasn't specific enough to act on. Checking their own rank history against the confirmed [causes of a rank drop](/blog/why-did-my-app-ranking-drop) found the real answer in about twenty minutes.

## What to actually do with this

1. **Treat rank as continuously moving**, check it regularly rather than waiting for a periodic review.
2. **Skip anything that violates either store's policy**, the durable path (keywords, stability, real reviews) is also the only one without removal risk.
3. **Give a new app or new keyword strategy weeks, not days**, before judging whether it's working.
4. **Look at install recency and velocity, not just your historical total**, when trying to understand a competitor's rank.
5. **Run ASO alongside paid, not instead of it**, [a rank tracker covering both your organic keyword positions and listing conversion](/rank) shows you where each channel is actually earning its keep.

The general answers to these questions are a reasonable starting point. The specific answer for your app is sitting in your own rank history, and that's the one worth checking before assuming the general case applies to you.
