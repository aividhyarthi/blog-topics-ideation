**Key points:**
- Google's own [October 13, 2026 developer post](https://developer.android.com/blog/posts/google-play-discovery-engagement-growth) confirms Ask Play can build dynamic, side-by-side app comparisons right in search results.
- Per Google's own words, it sums up your store listing and your reviews to answer a specific comparison question, like subscription plans or offline use.
- The same post says Play in Gemini has driven over 6 million app installs in two months since launch. Gemini Live now gives the same picks through voice too.
- There's no separate input for this. Your current listing text and your current reviews are the only material it has.

A shopper comparing two apps used to read both listings themselves. Google's own post confirms Ask Play can now do that work for them, built from what's already on your page.

## What Google's own post actually says

Per Google's own words, Ask Play "can generate dynamic, side-by-side app comparisons directly in search results." It "summarizes your store listing and user reviews" when someone asks about something specific. The post names subscription models and offline capabilities as examples.

| Detail | What Google's own post confirms |
|---|---|
| Where it shows up | Right in search results |
| What it compares | One specific thing, like plans or offline use |
| Where it pulls from | Your store listing and your reviews |
| Extra input needed | None. It works from what's already live |

## Why your listing just became comparison material

Your store listing's job just changed a bit. It was always written for a person reading it directly. Now it also feeds an AI that answers a shopper's exact question, stacked right up against a rival's listing and reviews.

A vague description gives Ask Play less to work with. Say your listing never mentions offline mode, but a rival's does. A user asking about offline support may see that gap stated plainly, pulled straight from the comparison.

> **Real-world scenario:** A productivity app's listing leaned on broad marketing words. It called itself "powerful and flexible" without naming real features. A rival's listing spelled out offline editing, export formats, and sync behavior in plain terms. Reading Google's own October post, the team saw the problem fast. A user asking Ask Play to compare the two on offline support would find far more to work with on the rival's page. They rewrote their own listing to name real features directly. That gave Ask Play something solid to pull from, instead of vague marketing lines.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Name real features in your listing, not just broad claims.** Per Google's own post, the comparison pulls from your actual text. Vague language gives it less to sum up.
2. **Treat your reviews as part of this too.** Google's own words name user reviews as source material, right alongside your own copy. Replying to reviews, and earning detailed ones, matters here too.
3. **Check your listing next to your real rivals.** If a rival's listing names something yours skips, a direct comparison may show that gap to anyone asking about it.
4. **Don't assume this runs everywhere yet.** Google's own post states the feature but gives it no rollout date. Confirm it's live before you build a whole strategy around it.
