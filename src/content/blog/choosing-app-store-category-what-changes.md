**Key points:**
- Apple's own [category page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/categories/) confirms every app gets two categories. "A primary and a secondary category."
- Per Apple's own words, the primary category decides what shows when people "browse the App Store or filter search results." It also decides which tab, Apps or Games, your app lands on.
- The secondary category matters on its own too. Entertainment or Games apps appear under Time Allowance. Kids apps get an extra discovery category.
- Apple's own guidance warns a mismatched category is a real review risk. Not just a weak choice. "Choosing categories that are not appropriate for your app is against the App Review Guidelines."

A dropdown menu takes thirty seconds to fill in during setup. It ends up deciding which tab your app lives on. And whether a browsing user ever sees it next to anything relevant.

## What Apple's own page actually confirms

Per Apple's own words, the primary category "will be the category in which the app appears when people browse the App Store or filter search results." It also "determines placement on the Apps tab or Games tab." That's not a cosmetic label. It's a real decision about where your app sits in the store.

| Main category's role | Browse spot, search filter, Apps/Games tab |

## Why the secondary category isn't just a backup slot

It's easy to treat the second category as an afterthought. Something filled in since the primary one already "does the real work." Apple's own page names real cases where it matters on its own. Entertainment and Games apps show up under the Time Allowance category. Whether picked as primary or secondary. That's the parental-control grouping behind screen time rules. A Kids app gets to add a second discovery category too. Games, Entertainment, or Education, on top of its Kids placement. Picking the secondary category carelessly means missing a real path. Not just leaving a field blank.

> **Real-world scenario:** A trivia-and-puzzle app launched under Games. The obvious fit. Its secondary category got left on a default setting, with no real thought behind it. Reading Apple's own guidance later, the team noticed something. Their app genuinely fit Entertainment too. It leaned heavily on social sharing and leaderboards, more than pure competitive play. They switched the secondary category to Entertainment. That didn't touch their primary placement. But it meant the app now showed up correctly in the Entertainment-specific groupings too. A path that had sat unused since launch.

## How Apple's own guidance says to actually pick one

Apple's own page gives three real questions. Not just a gut-feel pick. Your app's real job. The one word that fits its main use. Where users look for something like it. That means knowing your real crowd. Not guessing. Which spots already hold apps like yours. Users already know to check there. The page's own example: a photo-sharing social app could fit Photo & Video or Social Networking. The right call depends on which question points more clearly to your app's real core.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Treat your main category as a real placement choice.** Not a label. It sets your tab and your search filter directly.
2. **Give your second category real thought.** Especially for Entertainment, Games, or Kids apps. Apple's own page ties real paths to it.
3. **Run your pick through Apple's own three questions first.** Your real purpose. Where users look. What apps like yours already picked.
4. **Don't pick a category that doesn't fit, just because it looks busier.** Apple's own page ties this to a real review risk. Not just a weak choice.
