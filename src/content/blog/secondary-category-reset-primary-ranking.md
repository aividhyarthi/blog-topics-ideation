**Key points:**
- Your primary category's rank comes from signals tied to that category, not from how many categories you're also listed under.
- Adding a secondary category doesn't reset or reshuffle your existing standing in your primary one.
- A secondary category adds a second chance at visibility, a second list your app can show up in.
- A secondary category usually counts as a lighter signal than your primary one, not an equal identity.

A second category slot feels like it should touch your standing in the first one. Usually, it doesn't work that way.

## Why these are separate, not competing

Your primary category rank shows how your app does in that one category. It's built from signals like installs, use, and reviews tied to that category. Adding a second category doesn't rewrite or water down that history. It opens a second, extra list your app can show up in too. It runs next to your primary standing. It doesn't replace or reset any part of it.

| | What it actually does |
|---|---|
| Primary category | Your main identity, built from real signal history |
| Secondary category | Extra reach, added on top, not a replacement |

## Why the worry feels fair anyway

Adding something new to a setting with real history can feel risky. It feels like any change might shake something loose. Category choice doesn't act like that. Your primary category keeps its own built-up signal, no matter what else gets added next to it. The addition is exactly that. Extra. Not a swap.

> **Real-world scenario:** A team held off on a secondary category for months. They worried it might confuse the system or water down their standing in their primary one. When they finally added one, their primary rank stayed exactly where it had been. It still tracked the same signals it always had. What did change was a new, second list their app now showed up in too. Real extra reach they'd put off, out of a worry that never actually applied.

## Where the real tradeoff sits

A secondary category still deserves some thought though. It usually counts as a lighter signal than your primary one. Don't expect it to perform the same way. Picking a poor fit for a secondary category can also confuse a user browsing your listing, even without touching your primary rank at all. The real call is about fit. Not risk to what you've already built.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Don't let fear of a reset stop you from adding a secondary category.** That specific risk isn't how category ranking actually works.
2. **Pick a secondary category that's a real fit for your app.** The real cost of a bad pick is confusing a user, not a ranking hit.
3. **Don't expect your secondary category to match your primary one.** It's usually a lighter signal, extra reach rather than an equal claim.
4. **Treat it as a real shot at extra visibility.** A good secondary category is a real chance to be found somewhere new, at no cost to what you already have.
