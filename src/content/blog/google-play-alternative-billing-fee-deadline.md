**Key points:**
- Google's own [Play Console Help pages](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16470497?hl=en) confirm October 1, 2026 as the date reporting and fee duties start for US alternative billing and external content links programs.
- This only touches developers already enrolled in one of those two programs. Not every US developer.
- Google told enrolled developers directly on July 22, 2026. Real runway before the date lands.
- Reported per-install fees on a link-out download: $2.85 for an app, $3.65 for a game. Check your own exact numbers in Play Console.

If you're enrolled in Google Play's alternative billing or external content links program in the US, October 1, 2026 is a real date. Real obligations attached. Not a policy footnote. Here's what Google's own pages confirm, and what to do before it lands.

## What these two programs actually are

Google's own Play Console Help lays out both plainly. The [external content links program](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16470497?hl=en) lets a developer link US users out to buy in-app digital items outside Google Play Billing. Or to download an app whose installs Google Play doesn't manage at all. The [alternative billing program](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16497028?hl=en) lets a developer offer US users a different billing system for digital content. Alongside Google Play Billing, or instead of it.

Both exist because of the ongoing Epic Games v. Google case. Both are opt-in. Haven't enrolled in either? October 1 changes nothing for you directly.

## What actually changes on October 1

Enrolled developers start owing two things. Reporting. And payment. Report your transactions and successful downloads to Google. Pay the fee tied to them. This isn't a one-time signup step. It's an ongoing job from that date on, for as long as you stay enrolled.

The specific numbers floating around, $2.85 per app download and $3.65 per game download through a link-out, are worth treating as a reference point. Not gospel. Fee terms tied to active litigation can shift. Your own Play Console is the place to confirm your exact current figure before you build a financial plan around it.

> **Real-world scenario:** A subscription app enrolled in the external content links program earlier in 2026, hoping to route users to a cheaper direct-purchase flow. The team figured enrollment was the finish line. Nothing further required once approved. Reading Google's own page directly, ahead of October 1, showed a second phase. Real reporting and fee duties starting that date. They built the reporting step into their billing pipeline before the deadline hit. Not after a compliance notice forced their hand.

## What to actually do before October 1, 2026

1. **Confirm whether you're actually enrolled in either program.** This deadline only touches those two programs. Not every US developer on Google Play.
2. **Check your own Play Console for the exact current fee figures.** Outside numbers are a starting point. Not a stand-in for your own account's real terms.
3. **Build the reporting step into your pipeline now.** Not after October 1. This runs on and on, not a one-time box to tick.
4. **Re-check Google's own pages close to the date.** This whole framework sits inside active litigation. It's already shifted more than once in 2026.
