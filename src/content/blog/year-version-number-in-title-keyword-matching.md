**Key points:**
- A year in your title can catch real year-specific searches, but only for categories where users actually search that way.
- For most apps, that same space spent on a descriptive term is likely to earn more than a year does.
- A year-stamped title has an expiration built in. It needs updating or it starts reading as stale.
- The real call should track your category's actual search behavior, not a general guess that freshness always helps.

A year stamped into your app title looks like it should catch a wave of searches tied to that exact year. Whether it actually does depends a lot on what your app is.

## Where this genuinely helps

Some categories have real, searchable interest in recency. A tax app. A planner. Anything tied to an annual cycle where users want this year's version, not last year's. For those, a year in the title matches a real pattern in how people actually search. Not just a guess at what might help.

| | Does a year in the title likely help? |
|---|---|
| A category tied to a real annual cycle | Maybe, if users actually search that way |
| A category with no real yearly tie | Not much, and it costs real space |

## Why it's tempting even where it doesn't fit

A year feels like a free signal of freshness, cheap to add, seeming to say your app is current and well kept. But for an app with no real tie to an annual cycle, users searching for it mostly use plain terms with no year at all. Adding one there spends real, limited title space on a signal that doesn't match how people actually search for that kind of app.

> **Real-world scenario:** A team added the current year to their title, assuming it would read as fresh no matter what their app actually did. Checking how users actually found their app, almost none of the real search traffic used a year in any form. That space could have gone to a genuinely descriptive term instead, one that matched how people were actually searching. The year wasn't hurting anything, but it wasn't earning its place either, inside a title with real, tight limits on how much it could hold.

## What happens once the year passes

A year baked into a title carries its own expiration. Left alone, it eventually reads as outdated instead of current, the exact opposite of the fresh signal it was meant to send. That's a real maintenance job worth being honest about going in. A year you forget to update becomes a quiet weak spot sitting right in your title.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Check whether your category genuinely has year-specific search behavior first.** Not every app gains from this, even if the idea feels universal.
2. **Weigh a year against what else that same space could hold.** A genuinely descriptive term often earns more for most apps than a year does.
3. **Treat a year in your title as an ongoing job, not a one-time add.** It needs updating on time, or it works against the fresh signal it was meant to give.
4. **Check your real search data instead of just guessing.** Whether users actually search with a year is a plain, checkable fact for your own app, not a general rule.
