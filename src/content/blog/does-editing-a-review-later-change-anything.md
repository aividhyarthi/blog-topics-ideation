**Key points:**
- An edited review stays the same review. It doesn't become a new, separate entry.
- Your rating average reflects the current, latest state of every review, including ones a user has since changed.
- A user's edit is their own call entirely. You can ask, but you can't make a review change.
- Checking whether an old negative review has quietly improved is worth doing, since you won't notice unless you look.

A review you saw and replied to months ago isn't necessarily frozen exactly as it was that day.

## What actually happens when a review is edited

A user can go back to a review they left earlier and change it, the text, the rating, or both. When they do, it's still the same review. It doesn't get copied into a new entry sitting next to the old one. The original just gets updated, and your rating average going forward reflects that updated value in place of the old one, not on top of it.

| | What happens |
|---|---|
| User edits an old review | Same review, updated in place |
| Does it create a new entry? | No |
| Does your rating average reflect it? | Yes, the new value replaces the old one |

## Why this is easy to lose track of

A review feels like a fixed, permanent record once you've seen it and maybe replied to it. Most reviews probably stay exactly as written. But any single one could have quietly changed since you last checked, and nothing flags that a specific old review has been edited. That makes it easy to assume a review you looked at once and moved on from is still sitting exactly as it was, when it might not be.

> **Real-world scenario:** A team had replied publicly to a frustrated one-star review right after it was posted, answering the user's specific complaint and offering a fix. Months later, during an unrelated review check, they noticed that same review had been quietly bumped up to a much higher rating, with new text thanking the team for the fix. Nothing had told them it changed. Their old reply was still sitting there, a small loose end now slightly out of step with the better review next to it, one they only caught because they happened to look again.

## What this means for how you track reviews

A review isn't a locked snapshot once written. A regular look back at older reviews, especially ones you've already replied to, can turn up quiet changes you'd otherwise miss. It's also a reason not to give up on a bad review just because the moment to reply has passed. A user who later has a better experience, or who feels genuinely heard, sometimes does go back and update what they wrote, even with nothing forcing them to.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Don't assume an old review is frozen exactly as you last saw it.** Reviews can be edited in place, quietly, with no flag telling you it happened.
2. **Check back on reviews you've already replied to now and then.** It's the only way to catch a quiet edit you'd otherwise miss.
3. **Keep replying helpfully even to older bad reviews.** A genuinely useful reply can still lead a user to change what they wrote later, even without a guarantee.
4. **Don't try to push a user into editing a review.** It's entirely their call, same as writing it in the first place.
