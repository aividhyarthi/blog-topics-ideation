**Key points:**
- Google's own [October 13, 2026 blog post](https://developer.android.com/blog/posts/google-play-discovery-engagement-growth) confirms the Engage SDK now covers 9 categories. It names Education and Health & Fitness as new additions.
- Per Google's own words, the SDK works by showing personal content on Apps Home, the You tab, and Android tablets.
- A separate [eligibility page](https://developer.android.com/guide/playcore/engage/eligibility) lists 12 category names, not 9. The two pages don't agree, and neither says which is newer.
- This is a re-engagement tool. Not a way to find new users. It shows content only to people who already have your app.

A discovery surface most teams have never touched just opened up to more app categories. Whether yours qualifies depends on which Google page you check.

## What Google's own post actually confirms

Per Google's own words, from its October 13 checklist: "Integrate Engage SDK... now supporting 9 categories, including Education and Health & Fitness." It pairs that with one more line. The SDK works "by delivering personalized content across surfaces." Those surfaces: Apps Home. The You tab. Android tablets.

| Detail | What Google's own sources say |
|---|---|
| Category count, per the Oct 13 post | 9, naming Education and Health & Fitness |
| Category count, per the eligibility page | 12 named categories |
| Surfaces it populates | Apps Home, You tab, Android tablets |
| What it's for | Re-engagement, not new-user acquisition |

## Where the two sources actually disagree

Google's own eligibility page names 12 categories. Media & Entertainment. Books & Reference. Comics & Manga. News & Magazines. Music & Audio. Shopping. Food & Drink. Social. Travel & Local. Events. Health & Fitness. Education. That's 12, not 9. Neither page says which one is newer. Maybe the blog post undercounted. Maybe the eligibility page already reflects a later expansion. Neither source says.

One thing holds across both. Education and Health & Fitness are both confirmed, in either source. If your app sits in one of the other listed categories, check the Engage SDK program page directly. Don't trust either count alone.

> **Real-world scenario:** A meditation app's team saw the October 13 post. They assumed Health & Fitness being named meant they could integrate right away. They checked the Engage SDK program page first, before building anything. They found a real sign-up flow still gating access. Not an instant switch-on. They applied through that page, then scoped the real work only once their own eligibility was confirmed in writing. Not from a blog post's wording alone.

## Why this is a re-engagement tool, not a growth hack

Google's own words tie this to bringing users "back to your app." Not acquiring new ones. It populates surfaces a user already sees, because they already have your app installed. Apps Home. The You tab. Tablets. That's a different lever from anything touching search ranking or new-user discovery. Treat it as a retention tool worth having. Not a shortcut around ASO fundamentals.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Check your real status on Google's own program page.** A name match alone isn't proof, since the two sources don't agree.
2. **Treat this as retention, not growth.** It shows content to people who already have your app. Not new searchers.
3. **Keep your store listing fresh anyway.** Google's own post lists that as a separate, standing habit.
4. **Check back if you miss out today.** Google's own page has talked about more categories arriving "in the coming year." A miss now may not last.
