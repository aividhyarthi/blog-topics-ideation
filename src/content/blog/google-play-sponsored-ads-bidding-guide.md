**Key points:**
- Both the Sponsored tag in Play Store search, and ads inside suggested-for-you or Similar Apps rows, come from Google Ads App campaigns. Not the separate Display Network.
- Target cost-per-install (tCPI) is the bid strategy behind these placements. You set what you'll pay per install, not a flat rate per ad shown.
- One App campaign runs automatically across several surfaces at once: Play Store search, suggested rows, Google Search, YouTube, and Display.
- A budget too close to your bid limits how many auctions you enter. Google recommends a daily budget well above the bid itself.

You've seen the Sponsored tag next to a result in Play Store search. You've seen ads sitting inside a "you might also like" row too. Same product, or a different kind of bid?

## Same account, different inventory

Both placements come from one source: a Google Ads App campaign. Not the Display Network. Not a separate setup you configure on its own. One App campaign, aimed at driving installs, automatically enters your ad into auctions across several Google surfaces at once. Play Store search results. Suggested-for-you and Similar Apps rows. Google Search. YouTube. The regular Display Network too.

| Placement | Comes from |
|---|---|
| "Sponsored" tag in Play Store search | Google Ads App campaign |
| Ads in suggested-for-you / Similar Apps rows | Same App campaign |
| Regular Display Network banner ads | A different campaign type entirely |

## How the bidding actually works

Target cost-per-install, or tCPI, is the standard bid strategy for an App campaign built for installs. You set the amount you'll pay per install. Google's automated bidding system competes for you across every eligible surface, adjusting bids auction by auction to hit that target on average.

There's no separate, higher bid just for the Sponsored search slot. It's the same campaign, the same target, competing everywhere it's eligible. What actually changes your odds in any one auction is your bid competitiveness, your ad asset quality, and how well your app fits what that specific user seems to want.

Budget still sets a real ceiling. Google's own guidance for tCPI campaigns recommends a daily budget well above your target bid. The system needs enough room to run auctions and learn what converts. A budget set too close to the bid limits how many auctions you can even enter, Play Store search included.

> **Real-world scenario:** A team assumed getting into the Sponsored search slot meant building a separate, higher-budget campaign just for it. After checking how App campaigns actually route ads, they found their existing tCPI campaign already competed for that slot on its own. Instead of a new campaign, they raised their daily budget well above the recommended threshold and added two more ad asset variations. Their Sponsored search appearances rose without any separate setup.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Don't build a separate campaign just for Sponsored search.** Your existing App campaign with tCPI bidding already competes for it.
2. **Set your budget well above your target cost-per-install.** A tight budget limits every auction you enter, not just search.
3. **Add more ad asset variations if you're running a thin set.** More assets mean more auction eligibility across every placement.
4. **Track installs against your actual bid, not just impressions.** High impressions with weak conversions points to an asset problem, not a bidding one.
