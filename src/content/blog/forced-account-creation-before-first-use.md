**Key points:**
- A sign-up wall placed before any real use adds a step between install and the moment a user might actually want to stay.
- Users who haven't seen value yet have the least reason to hand over an email or create a password.
- Some apps genuinely need an account early. The real question is whether the ask comes before or after a reason to say yes.
- Letting someone reach a real "aha" moment first, then asking for an account, tends to convert better than asking up front.

An account wall is often the very first screen after install. That's also the moment a brand-new user has the least invested in your app and the easiest excuse to just leave.

## Why the timing matters more than the ask itself

Creating an account isn't a huge task on its own. The problem is where it sits. Right after install, a user hasn't done anything yet. They haven't seen what the app actually does for them. Asking for an email, a password, or a social login at that exact moment means asking for trust before you've earned any. Some users will do it anyway. A real share won't, and that share never gets past screen one.

| | What the user has seen | What they're being asked for |
|---|---|---|
| Account wall before first use | Nothing yet | Trust, with no evidence behind it |
| Account ask after a real action | A real reason to want it | Trust, backed by something they just experienced |

## When an account is actually needed early

Some apps genuinely can't work without one from the start, like something built entirely around data synced across a user's own devices. That's a real case, not an excuse. The point isn't that accounts are always wrong. It's that most apps default to asking early out of habit, not because the core experience actually requires it on screen one.

> **Real-world scenario:** A team required account creation as the very first screen, assuming it was a small, standard step every app has anyway. Their install-to-active numbers told a different story: a real share of new installs never got past that screen at all. Moving the account ask to after the user completed one small real action inside the app, instead of before it, let far more people see what the app actually did before being asked to commit to anything.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Check how much of your app actually needs an account from screen one.** Many apps assume this without really testing it.
2. **Let a user hit one real moment of value before asking.** That's the point where saying yes to an account actually makes sense to them.
3. **Watch your install-to-active numbers around this exact screen.** A steep drop right where the wall sits is a clear, checkable signal.
4. **Keep the delayed ask specific.** Tie it to something real, like saving progress, not a generic "create an account to continue."
