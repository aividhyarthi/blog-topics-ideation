**Key points:**
- Apple's own [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), section 4.3(b), name specific categories at risk. Dating, flashlight, sound effects, wallpaper, simple timers, and fortune telling apps.
- A new submission in these categories gets rejected unless it offers a meaningfully different or improved experience. Not a small tweak. A real difference.
- Existing apps aren't safe either. Apple's own wording allows removal if an app in these categories isn't updated, improved, or doesn't attract customers.
- [9to5Mac reported](https://9to5mac.com/2026/06/09/apple-tightens-app-review-guidelines-against-apps-that-do-not-add-value-to-the-app-store/) tighter enforcement of this guideline starting June 2026.

A simple utility app used to be an easy way onto the App Store. A flashlight. A timer. A wallpaper picker. Apple now names these kinds of apps directly. The bar to stay listed just got real. Here's the exact wording. Not a paraphrase.

## What Apple's own guidelines actually say

Section 4.3 is titled Spam. Sub-section 4.3(b) is direct:

> "Don't submit apps that are indistinguishable from what's already widely available. Opportunistically creating variants of existing app categories or popular apps degrades App Store discovery, reduces overall app quality, and harms both users and developers. Certain kinds of apps, such as dating, flashlight, sound effects, wallpaper, simple timers, and fortune telling, are well established on the App Store and we will not accept new submissions unless they offer a meaningfully different or improved experience. We may remove these apps from the App Store going forward if they are not updated, improved, or do not attract customers."

That's Apple's own text, word for word, from its [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). Six named categories. A clear bar. Meaningfully different or improved. And a real consequence for apps already live, not just new ones trying to get in.

| Question | What Apple's own guideline confirms |
|---|---|
| Categories named directly | Dating, flashlight, sound effects, wallpaper, simple timers, fortune telling |
| New submission bar | Meaningfully different or improved experience required |
| Existing app risk | Can be removed if not updated, improved, or if it doesn't attract customers |
| Separate, harsher category | Drinking games, Kama Sutra, fart, and burp apps, called low-effort |
| Repeat offense consequence | Can lead to removal from the Apple Developer Program entirely |

## Why this matters right now

[9to5Mac's June 2026 reporting](https://9to5mac.com/2026/06/09/apple-tightens-app-review-guidelines-against-apps-that-do-not-add-value-to-the-app-store/) confirms this. Apple is enforcing the rule harder now. Not just leaving it on the books. The rule itself isn't new. What changed is how hard Apple applies it. Picture an old flashlight app. Quiet. Unchanged for years. Living off old downloads. That's exactly the kind of app this targets. Rank and downloads don't protect it. The rule is plain. Skip an update, and that alone can be a reason for removal.

> **Real-world scenario:** A small team ran a simple wallpaper app for years. Steady, if modest, downloads. They figured a stable app with real users was safe from review scrutiny. Reading Apple's own rule directly changed their mind. Their app hadn't seen a real update in over a year. Instead of waiting to find out the hard way, they added something genuinely new. AI wallpaper generation from a text prompt. Their old version couldn't do that at all. That's the kind of real difference the rule asks for. Not a new icon. Not a copy change.

## What to actually do if this applies to you

1. **Check your app against the exact list.** Not a vague gut feeling. Dating, flashlight, sound effects, wallpaper, simple timers, fortune telling. Named directly. If your app is one of these, the rule applies to you.
2. **Name your "meaningfully different" feature in one clear sentence.** A real function, not a fresh coat of paint. Apple's own words draw that line.
3. **Don't assume an old, stable app is safe.** The rule covers apps already live too. Tied to whether you keep them updated and whether people still want them.
4. **Read section 4.3 in full before your next submission.** This piece covers just 4.3(b). The wider spam section has more worth knowing first.
