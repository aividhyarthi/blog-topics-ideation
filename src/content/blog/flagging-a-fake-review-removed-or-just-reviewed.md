**Key points:**
- Flagging a review submits it for review. It's not an instant removal.
- The review stays up, at full weight, for however long that review takes.
- A review only comes down if the store agrees it actually breaks policy, not just because it's negative.
- A public reply is still worth posting while a flag is pending, since it gives context in the meantime.

Hitting "report" on a review that looks fake feels like pulling a trigger. What actually happens next is slower and far less certain than that.

## Why flagging isn't the same as removing

Flagging a review starts a review process on the store's side. It doesn't take the review down on the spot. The review keeps counting toward your average, full weight, for as long as that review takes, which can be longer than most teams expect. Treating a flag as "handled" the moment you submit it is the most common mistake here.

| | What happens right away |
|---|---|
| Flagging a review | Submitted for review. Still counts, still visible. |
| A confirmed policy violation | Removed, once the store actually agrees. |

## Why "fake" or "unfair" isn't enough on its own

The store removes a review only if it actually breaks a real policy: being fake, spam, abusive, or similarly against the rules. A genuine but harsh review you simply disagree with doesn't qualify, no matter how unfair it feels. This is the gap that catches teams off guard. Flagging every negative review as if being negative alone were grounds for removal just means a lot of flags that go nowhere.

> **Real-world scenario:** A team flagged a cluster of suspicious-looking reviews that had appeared within the same day, assuming the obvious pattern alone would get them pulled quickly. Weeks passed with the reviews still live and still counting. A closer read of the actual policy showed the pattern alone wasn't enough. Submitting specific evidence of the coordinated timing, rather than just flagging each review individually, eventually got the cluster reviewed and removed.

## What to actually do about it

1. **Don't treat a flag as a done deal.** The review stays up and counts until the store actually acts on it.
2. **Flag based on an actual policy violation, not just a low rating.** A real violation is what gets something removed, not unfairness alone.
3. **Give the store something specific to act on.** Real evidence of a pattern tends to move faster than a bare report with no context.
4. **Reply publicly while you wait.** It's worth doing regardless of how the flag eventually resolves.
